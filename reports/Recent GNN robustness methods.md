# Papers harden GNNs with separate mechanisms

Between 2023 and 2026, and most clearly in the 2024–2026 papers that were actually opened, graph neural networks are being made more robust by changing which edges carry a message, by certifying a prediction inside a counted edit budget, or by rebuilding features or structure at test time. These are separate mechanisms. The propagation rule in active use is robust aggregation: **RUNG** zeros an edge once a normalized feature gap crosses a minimax-concave threshold and otherwise reweights that edge inside unrolled layers, which is edge reweighting during message passing keyed to a feature residual ([Hou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14934)). Structural scores such as Jaccard, common neighbors, and degree gap show up in frozen preprocessing, notably **TAGR**, before an unmodified GNN runs ([Goel and Kanniainen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03462)). Certified defenses that were opened prove stability under a number of edge edits, injected nodes, or arbitrarily rewritten feature vectors, and **SpecSphere** is the paper that turns a Lipschitz constant of the propagator itself into a joint edge-and-feature certificate ([Choi, Choi, and Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)). A training penalty on how far node states move when edges are deleted was not found. Text-attributed defenses use language-model judgments or cached embedding cosines as keep-or-drop tests; none of the papers that were read multiplies a structural weight by a frozen text score or names a structural trust budget ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685); [Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)). The record behind these claims is OpenAlex, arXiv HTML, and PDFs retrieved on 4 October 2026. Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 on every query, a later arXiv batch also returned HTTP 429, several abstracts were missing, and several venues were never printed on the pages that were read.

## Feature-gap reweighting is what propagation papers train

The 2024–2026 edge defenses decide trust in a few recurring ways, and only one of them reweights inside aggregation. **RUNG** treats message passing as robust mean estimation. An ℓ1 penalty is described as biased once outliers accumulate, so the authors replace it with a minimax concave penalty and unroll a quasi-Newton iteratively reweighted least-squares solver as aggregation layers on an APPNP-style stack. The edge weight is the derivative of that penalty and becomes zero when the normalized feature difference meets or exceeds a threshold γ, which prunes the edge. A separate smoothing weight controls how strongly the estimate stays near the original features. The notes record that weight through the reparameterization λ̂ = 1/(1+λ), tuned in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9}, while γ is tuned in {0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5}. That split is the nearest published version of “which neighbor” versus “how much influence,” and the discrepancy that drives it is a **feature gap**, recovered from the node states being estimated ([Hou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)). Crossref indexes a proceedings version as Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37 (2024); the arXiv PDF’s first page has no venue banner.

Under adaptive local evasion on Cora-ML, the same paper’s Table 1 is the head-to-head a reader needs. Clean accuracy, then budgets of 100% and 200% of the target degree, are **84.0±5.3 / 70.7±10.6 / 69.3±9.0** for RUNG. The same columns are **2.0±2.7** for GNNGuard at 200%, **0.0** for ProGNN and RGCN, **0.7±1.3** for Jaccard-GCN, **8.7±3.4** for SoftMedian, and **12.0±6.9** for TWIRLS. On a global 40% edge budget the gap shrinks and the order holds: RUNG **65.1±1.2**, SoftMedian **58.1±0.7**, GNNGuard **51.0±1.2**, ProGNN **51.3±0.6**, Jaccard-GCN **49.1±2.4**, GCN **48.4±0.5**. Poisoning at 40% on Cora-ML is **64.6±1.3** for RUNG against **48.7±1.0** for GCN. A Citeseer graph-injection appendix moves RUNG-MCP from **75.65** to **51.13**, against GNNGuard from **73.47** to **34.73**; standard deviations for that table were not in the extract. Hou et al. group SoftMedian, TWIRLS, and ElasticGNN as the ℓ1 family whose bias RUNG is built to remove. ElasticGNN has no numeric row in the extracted Tables 1–2. RUNG’s own complexity claim is O((m+n)kd) with a diagonal Hessian approximation, presented as still efficient. Extracted experiments are Cora-ML and Citeseer. Limitations on heterophily, and on graphs larger than Citeseer, were not in the extracted pages, which is silence in those pages and not a proof the paper never discusses them.

Other 2024–2026 trust rules repair the graph and then freeze it. **GPR-GAE** (Lee and Park, arXiv 2026; no venue printed on the PDF) trains a self-supervised graph autoencoder with several Generalized PageRank filters, purifies the adjacency in multiple steps, and trains the classifier on the cleaned graph. Trust is the reconstructed edge set. Hop mix is a separate learned coefficient, so radius and edge existence are factored, which is a coarser split than RUNG’s γ versus λ. Training corruption uses edge drops, additions, and reweighting at a reported budget (1.5, 0.2, 3) with K=7. On Cora, adversarially trained GPRGNN under PRBCD with training ε=0.2 reaches **48.5%** test accuracy against PRBCD at ε=0.5, and GPR-GAE attached to vanilla GPRGNN is **15.7 points** higher. GPR-GAE plus vanilla GCN is **79.4±0.3** clean and **61.3±3.6** under PRBCD at ε=0.5. Transfer (non-adaptive) Citeseer at clean / ε=0.25 / ε=0.5 is **71.2 / 68.7 / 66.4** against vanilla GCN **71.5 / 40.0 / 25.3**. Adaptive gradients are described as unscalable on OGB-arXiv, so that graph is transfer-only, and SVD-GCN is marked out of memory there ([Lee and Park](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)). **TAGR** (Goel and Kanniainen, arXiv 2 June 2026; no venue on the PDF) is the method whose score is explicitly structural. Before training it builds a sparse feature-Gaussian neighborhood and reweights each observed edge by a fixed mix, q_ij = 1.50 z(cosine) + 0.50 z(Jaccard) + 0.25 z(log(1+M_ij)) + 0.10 z(κ_i+κ_j) − 0.10 z(|d_i−d_j|), then freezes that adjacency for an unmodified GNN. Feature agreement has the largest coefficient. Setting the residual multiplier to zero recovers Gaussian-only repair, which the authors call the main gain; Cora-ML deletion gains over that Gaussian-only graph are **0.7 points** at 25% deletion and **0.3 points** at 50%. Clean Cornell, a standard heterophilous graph, is **0.8203±0.0581** for TAGR-GCN against **0.2270±0.0308** for GCN, reported clean only. The extracted threat is random edge addition and deletion. The repaired graph cannot adapt to an attacker who sees the repair ([Goel and Kanniainen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)).

**GJDNet** (Cui et al., arXiv 1 June 2026; generic LaTeX template, peer-reviewed venue unverified) filters neighbors that are skewness outliers in feature similarity and, separately, tightens class regions with a spherical decision boundary. On its Cora “min-max” table the accuracies at perturbation rates 0 / 0.10 / 0.25 are **0.8564 / 0.8329 / 0.7770**, against GCN-Jaccard **0.7422** and RGCN **0.7450** at rate 0.25. That margin is a few points. Whether the attack matches the adaptive PGD protocol in Mujkanovic et al. was not checked. Rejection-perturbation coverage under a 5% min-max attack includes heterophilous graphs, with Actor the weakest printed coverage at **45.91%**; what that coverage counts was not re-derived ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)).

The rest of the slice is real and thinner. **ADEdgeDrop** (IEEE TKDE typesetting; volume and issue year not extracted) trains an adversarial predictor on the line graph to choose edges to drop; a toy figure reports GCN+ADEdgeDrop at **84.3** accuracy with 252 edges, and the table flags one-day timeouts and 16G out-of-memory ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09171)). **GRANCE** (AAAI 2025) refines neighborhoods with dual contrastive learning plus a neighbor estimator ([Zhuang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i12.33471)). **Pure-GNN** keeps every edge and changes importance weights plus a residual, so “which neighbor” stays the observed graph ([Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.26599/tst.2025.9010034)). **DSGNN** runs one channel for structural noise and one for feature noise; the abstract’s highest averages are **81.24%** (Cora), **71.94%** (CiteSeer), and **81.66%** (Industry), and the fusion formula was not retrieved ([Chen et al.](https://doi.org/10.32604/cmc.2025.067284)). **BDP** deletes low-Jaccard edges on bipartite graphs and reconnects implicit edges ([Deng et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn60899.2024.10650844)). Tong et al. score injected edges with link prediction plus a homogeneity filter, with no accuracy number in the abstract ([Tong et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/dsc63484.2024.00031)). Main-table numbers were not extracted for most of these, and no 2024–2026 paper in the pass reports one shared leaderboard of RUNG against GPR-GAE, GJDNet, and DSGNN. Adaptive local PGD, PRBCD, random deletion, and a min-max rate are different experiments. Mujkanovic, Geisler, Günnemann, and Bojchevski, evaluating seven defenses, report that of 49 robust-GNN papers at major venues essentially none used adaptive attacks, that non-adaptive attacks make the same defenses look robust, and that on Cora-ML about half the defenses are worse than an undefended GCN under the strongest global attack at a given budget. Hou et al. and Lee and Park cite that paper as 2022; OpenAlex records the arXiv copy as 2023; the NeurIPS 2022 proceedings page was not opened ([Mujkanovic et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13694)).

A propagation rule that reweights edges from **structural** discrepancy therefore has a partial match and a clear miss. RUNG reweights during propagation, and the quantity that crosses the threshold is a normalized feature difference, with λ setting residual trust in the original features. TAGR puts structural discrepancy in the score — degree gap, Jaccard, common neighbors — and then stops: the adjacency is frozen before training, and the largest coefficient is still feature cosine. GNNGuard, as restated in Mujkanovic et al.’s appendix, keeps edges whose layer-wise embedding cosine is at least 0.1 and rescales by a degree-like normalizer. That is an embedding gate during propagation, recovered from a 2023 evaluation paper, and the original GNNGuard formula was not re-read. The heterophily survey’s taxonomy already has a slot called identifiable message aggregation, split into edge weight, feature weight, or hybrid weight, but that survey organizes heterophily architectures. Its readable text is the ar5iv HTML of arXiv:2202.07082; OpenAlex lists a 2026 IEEE TKDE record with 107 citations ([Zheng et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.07082)). **ES-GNN** (IEEE TPAMI 2024, 22 OpenAlex citations) splits edges into two complementary subgraphs and also claims adversarial-graph robustness ([Guo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2024.3459932)). Edge splitting and a hard Jaccard cutoff are membership rules. They do not continuously reweight from a structural discrepancy inside the aggregator.

## Certificates count edits while Lipschitz control sits on the filter or the head

Set beside a Lipschitz penalty on how much node states move when edges are deleted, the opened 2024–2026 papers constrain a different object. When they want a guarantee, they change the input distribution or the aggregator. Orthogonal weight constraints and spectral normalization of GNN layers were not found as training methods for certificates. The arXiv query `"spectral normalization" AND "graph neural"` returned 0 hits, as did `"sparse randomized smoothing"` and `"weight norm" AND Lipschitz AND "graph neural"`. A second arXiv batch then returned HTTP 429, so those zero hits are consistent with the opened sample and are not a proof that no such paper exists.

**SpecSphere** (Choi, Choi, and Kim; arXiv 13 May 2025, HTML of v3 dated 1 August 2025; venue not stated on the pages read) is the paper that puts a Lipschitz constant on the propagator and turns it into a certificate. The certified object is the infinity-norm change of the fused node representation under a joint threat set with ∥ΔA∥₂ ≤ √(2p) and an ℓ∞ feature perturbation. The spectral branch uses a Chebyshev expansion on a rescaled Laplacian: ∥T_k∥₂ ≤ 1 and ∥T_k(L̃+Δ) − T_k(L̃)∥₂ ≤ k 2^{k−1} ∥Δ∥₂, which produces a branch bound (2^{K+1}−1)∥c∥₁ times either ∥X∥∞ or 1. The spatial branch assumes each attention layer has weight spectral norm at most β and ∥∂Â/∂A∥₂ ≤ 1. A gate composes the branches. Training described on the page is branch-specific adversarial inner maximization, low-pass and high-pass regularizers, and a consistency penalty. In one heterophilic setting λ_adv = 1.0 and λ_cons = 10^{−1}; the authors say λ_cons < 10^{−3} fails to align the branches and λ_cons ≥ 1.0 blocks adaptive fusion. Whether ∥W∥₂ ≤ β is enforced by spectral normalization, by projection, or only assumed in the proof was not visible in the extracted sections ([Choi, Choi, and Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)).

The other Lipschitz objects in this window are architectural or analytic. **FSW-GNN** (accepted at the Fourth Learning on Graphs Conference, LoG 2025) makes the message-passing embedding bi-Lipschitz with respect to the doubly stochastic metric and Tree Mover’s Distance by replacing sum aggregation with a Fourier sliced-Wasserstein map. The page states that standard MPNNs are not lower-Lipschitz, which is why Weisfeiler–Leman-separable graphs can land on nearly identical embeddings. The stated scope is graphs of bounded size with continuous, bounded node features. This is separation stability, covering oversmoothing and oversquashing, and the page does not present it as an adversarial ℓ2 or edge-edit certificate ([Sverdlov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09118)). Davidson and Dym (arXiv 11 June 2024; venue not stated) show uniform upper Lipschitzness for the parametric MPNNs they consider and a lower Hölder-in-expectation exponent for sum aggregation; SortMPNN is their lower-Lipschitz-in-expectation construction. FSW-GNN later says its proof also shows SortMPNN is bi-Lipschitz in the standard sense, and that SortMPNN pads neighborhoods to a fixed maximum size and does not support non-integer edge weights ([Davidson and Dym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06984)). **mHC-GNN** (Mishra, arXiv 5 January 2026; venue not stated) projects stream-mixing matrices onto doubly stochastic matrices with Sinkhorn–Knopp. It is not an adversarial certificate. Removing that projection is described as deterministic collapse, with Chameleon **18.20±0.00**, Texas **10.81±0.00**, and Cora Full **13.00±0.00**, “up to 82% degradation” ([Mishra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02451)).

**STEM-GNN**, “Generalizing GNNs with Tokenized Mixture of Experts” (Guo et al.; arXiv 9 February 2026; comment: accepted to KDD 2026), is the case where a penalty does bound a Lipschitz constant and the constant is the wrong one for an edge-deletion state penalty. The enforced quantity is the prediction head: a Frobenius penalty, cited by the authors to Miyato et al. (2018), written as the stability constraint L_h η_θ ρ ≤ ε. The admissible set includes both a feature budget ρ_x and a structure budget ρ_s. Message passing enters the theory as graph filters that split a low-frequency and a high-frequency component. Theorem 3.1 says a Lipschitz stability constraint caps reliance on the high component and leaves a nonzero worst-environment floor. The encoder is a mixture-of-experts message passer plus vector quantization, and those parts are not described as spectrally normalized. The argument is stability under frozen deployment: a cap on the head, inside an admissible set that already includes a structure budget. Node-state movement under edge deletion lies outside that penalty, and the page does not issue a radius inside which the prediction is constant. The arXiv API abstract says “nine” benchmarks; the HTML introduction says “eight.” Both wordings were on the pages read ([Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09258)). Sun and Lin (arXiv 6 February 2024; venue not stated) put the spectral norm of the feature diffusion matrix ∥P_G∥₂ inside a robust PAC-Bayesian generalization gap, together with weight norms and a perturbation intensity ε. They write that the bounds do not have a significant effect on the graph edge attack, and the bound does not extend to node classification because those samples are not independent ([Sun and Lin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04038)). Liang et al. (arXiv 4 June 2026; venue not stated) tighten a robust PAC-Bayesian margin bound for message-passing GNNs and say local adversarial perturbations are amplified by message passing. The exact admissible set was not fully visible, so feature ball versus edge edits stays unverified beyond the introduction. This is an analysis bound, not a training constraint ([Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06293)). The closest training penalty on sensitivity to graph perturbation is outside node classification: Schmidt and Mateos penalize the spatial Lipschitz constant of a GNN-parameterized flow so that relative graph perturbations move the generated distribution less. The note records the paper as submitted to IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing (arXiv 8 July 2026) ([Schmidt and Mateos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07510)).

What the opened defenses actually certify is combinatorial. **AuditVotes** (Lai et al.; arXiv 29 March 2025, v3 updated 22 July 2026; comment: accepted by CCS 2026) issues a randomized-smoothing radius in graph edits and evaluates certified accuracy under an edge-attack budget. It majority-votes randomized graphs, rewires each draw with Jaccard, a feature autoencoder, or multi-head feature similarity, and drops low-confidence votes. On Cora-ML under 20-edge attacks, clean accuracy improves by **437.1%** and certified accuracy by **409.3%** relative to their vanilla-smoothing baseline. On Citeseer, edge-insertion attacks up to 20 edges, SimAug moves clean accuracy from **14.7%** to **70.9%**, and SimAug plus the confidence filter moves certified accuracy from **14.7%** to **72.6%**. Robust accuracy is **83.3%** on Cora-ML at 5 edges per node. Applied as a plug-in to GNNCert, which was not itself opened, SimAug improves clean accuracy by **12.7%** and certified accuracy by **13.9%** on Cora-ML. Those percents are the paper’s reported changes, not radii. The authors say raising the edge-noise rate to enlarge the radius destroys clean accuracy ([Lai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22998)). A collective certificate (Lai et al., arXiv 3 March 2024; venue not stated) lower-bounds the fraction of a target set whose predictions stay fixed under one shared injected graph, using node-aware bi-smoothing attributed to Lai et al. (2023) — each edge deleted with probability p_e, each node with probability p_n — then a linear-program relaxation. On Citeseer the certified ratio moves from **0.0%** to **81.2%** when injected nodes are 5% of the graph. Injected nodes have arbitrary features and each adds at most τ edges. The 2023 bi-smoothing paper was not opened ([Lai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01423)).

**AGNNCert** (Li and Wang; arXiv 2 February 2025; comment: accepted by USENIX Security 2025) certifies a deterministic size: a threshold on the total number of manipulated nodes, nodes whose features are manipulated, and edges. A hash partitions the graph so one edge edit changes one subgraph vote, and majority vote is the predictor. A feature change is counted per node, with the rewrite unrestricted inside that count. It is not an ℓ2 or ℓ∞ radius. The authors say an edge-centric certificate is dominated by the number of edges incident to manipulated nodes, so one injected high-degree node can exceed the certified size, and a naive node partition gives node classification only one vote and “vacuous robustness.” The node-centric directed partition is their stated fix ([Li and Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00765)). **PGNNCert** uses the same hash-and-vote construction for training-time poisoning (arXiv 24 March 2025; comment: accepted at CVPR 2025). No certified-accuracy percentage was copied from the HTML that was read ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18503)). Message-interception smoothing (Scholten et al.; arXiv 5 January 2023, accepted at NeurIPS 2022, HTML header showing a 10 November 2024 revision stamp) is included because later certificates cite it. The defender’s noise randomly deletes edges and ablates all features of random nodes, then bounds the chance that a message from a controlled node reaches the target. The attacker arbitrarily rewrites features of at most ρ whole nodes. Edge deletion here is smoothing noise. It is not a penalty on state displacement. Certificates on Cora-ML are reported in **17 seconds**, versus up to **25 minutes** for Bojchevski et al. (2020). No certified-accuracy percentage was copied ([Scholten et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02039)).

No opened 2024–2026 paper states a certified radius equal to the Lipschitz constant of a GraphSAGE or GCN message function under an ℓ2 feature ball alone, and none states a training penalty whose term is the movement of node states under edge deletion. GNNCert (cited as Xia et al. 2024) and RES-GRACE (cited as Lin et al. 2023, and by SpecSphere as a single-branch (ℓ0, ℓ∞) certificate) were not opened because of the arXiv rate limit. Identifying a Li and Wang 2025 citation inside AuditVotes with AGNNCert is an inference from author names and year, not a citation that was seen on the page. Continuous feature-ℓ2 certification of the image-domain kind was not found for graphs in the opened papers; AuditVotes mentions Gaussian smoothing for images.

## Text scores delete edges, and attacks already aim at that gate

The third comparison — frozen text scores that allocate a structural trust budget — meets a hard gate and stops. No paper in the set that was read multiplies a structural edge weight or an attention coefficient by a text similarity, and none uses the phrase “trust budget.” Semantic scores spend the entire edge or none of it.

Three 2025–2026 defenses do treat node text, or embeddings of that text, as evidence for which edges to trust after a structural attack, and all three still assume the text itself was not rewritten. **LLM4RGNN** (Zhang et al.; arXiv August 2024; comment: accepted by KDD 2025) first shows that a stronger text encoder is not a topology defense. Six LLM or LM feature setups under Mettack at a 20% perturbation rate on Cora and PubMed suffer a maximum accuracy decrease of **37.9%** and an average decrease of **23.1%**. Vanilla GCN on the same check has a maximum decrease of **39.1%** and an average of **35.5%**. The repair then prompts GPT-4, on a clean graph, for maliciousness judgments and analyses of **26,518** edges, LoRA-tunes a local LLM (Mistral-7B or Llama3-8B), and distills those labels into an edge MLP on concatenated LM features. The MLP adds an edge when its probability exceeds γ, tuned in {0.91, 0.93, 0.95, 0.97, 0.99}. If no edges would be removed, a purification threshold β is chosen from {1, 2}; otherwise from {2, 3, 4}. Cosine similarity builds a negative candidate pool, the 4,000 lowest-similarity pairs. It is not a multiplier on the adjacency. GPT-4 is not queried on the attacked graph at training time. On OGBN-Arxiv (**169,343** nodes, **1,166,243** edges) the local LLM caches inferred edges and the quadratic predictor runs only on the top-K neighbors per node. The abstract says that in some cases, at a 40% perturbation ratio, GNN accuracy with this repair is still better than accuracy on the clean graph. An “Impact of Text Quality” section exists; its numbers were not extracted. This is a fine-tuned pipeline, not a frozen text score ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)).

**Guardual**, in the TGRB paper (HTML byline Runlin Lei, Lu Yi, Mingguo He, Pengyu Qiu, and Zhewei Wei, with Yongchao Liu named in a footnote; arXiv 20 October 2025, 2510.17185; full author list and venue were not recovered after a later HTTP 429), replaces GNNGuard’s single validation-tuned threshold with two similarity thresholds on a precomputed embedding. The listed thresholds are for RoBERTa. Training chooses a conservative threshold by score = 0.7 · P_preserve + 0.3 · P_filter, where P_preserve is the fraction of intra-class edges retained and P_filter is the fraction of inter-class edges removed. Test uses equal 0.5 weights. Those 0.7 and 0.3 weights choose the threshold. They are not edge multipliers. Table 12 training/test thresholds include Cora **0.250/0.503**, PubMed **0.360/0.633**, and ArXiv **0.320/0.540**, with several datasets at a training threshold of **0.000**. The paper says the largest gains over GNNGuard are on Computer and Photo, **+8.23%** and **+10.74%**. The same paper reports that GNNGuard becomes “top-tier” against inductive and evasion structural attacks once node features are advanced text embeddings, and that earlier robust-GNN papers often evaluated only shallow embeddings. The excerpt does not write an updated edge of the form A_ij · cos(text_i, text_j). Whether TGRB inherits a cosine edge coefficient from the 2020 GNNGuard formula and only retunes the threshold was not on the extracted pages, so that inheritance is not claimed ([Lei et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)).

**SFT-auto**, in the same TGRB paper, flags a node as structure-attacked if its embedding cosine is below **0.5** to more than half its neighbors, then keeps that node’s own text and removes low-similarity connections. If the text itself is flagged as attacked, the center text is discarded and the model uses neighbor text. Own text is the reliable evidence under a structure attack, and it is not trusted under a text attack. The LLM is supervised-fine-tuned. Cost is one LLM forward pass per sample, not per edge. The IEEE S&P 2026 purification (Ma, Wang, and Yan; arXiv 27 March 2026; comment: to appear at IEEE S&P 2026) drops an edge when cosine similarity of the LLM or LM embeddings of the two endpoints falls below a threshold. The operation described is deletion. Freeze status of the eight feature enhancers was not stated in the sections read. On GCN under non-targeted structural attacks the authors call the defense “highly effective” relative to one input-level and one model-level defense; specific table numbers and any numeric relative drop were not extracted. The embeddings are whatever the enhancer already produced, so there is no per-edge LLM call ([Ma, Wang, and Yan](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26105)). GraphTextack’s threat model says the text map may be a frozen LLM encoder, a fine-tuned model, or another encoder. That sentence describes the victim, not a trust-budget defense ([Ma, Trivedi, and Koutra](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12423)).

Attacks already target these gates and, separately, feature-residual reweighting. TGRB configures **PGD-Guard**, citing Mujkanovic et al. (2022), as an adaptive structural attack that only perturbs node pairs whose cosine exceeds a threshold, “emulating defense-aware strategies intended to bypass similarity filtering (targeting GNNGuard).” Thresholds searched are {0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, the budget is 20% of edges, and the target embedding is RoBERTa. That is embedding-camouflaged edge edits. It is not described as a lexical or BM25 constraint. **BadGraph** (Chen et al.; arXiv 22 March 2026; comment: accepted by TheWebConf 2026) jointly edits adjacency and node text, keeps partial lexical content under an L0 token budget, and reports accuracy cuts of up to **76.3%** across GNN and LLM reasoners. Under SBERT it reduces RUNG’s accuracy by **over 50%**, which the authors attribute to semantically aligned edges being up-weighted. RUNG “employs edge reweighting to prune suspicious edges,” and BadGraph’s perturbations cause that reweighting to “overemphasize adversarial edges.” The excerpt does not say RUNG’s weight is a text similarity ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)). **GraphTextack** (AAAI 2026) injects nodes whose structure and text are searched together; the sections that were read do not describe a lexical edge ranker. A strict threat model that chooses the injected edge because the two texts overlap lexically, or because a BM25 score is high, was not found. BM25, token overlap, and inverted-index edge filters were absent from the method sections that were read. That is a negative from this pass. The 2024 text-level graph-injection paper that TGRB cites as Lei, Hu, Ren, and Wei (NeurIPS 2024) had its title and arXiv id truncated, and a retry returned HTTP 429, so whether that paper ranks edges by lexical similarity is unknown here.

**GraphEdit** fine-tunes an LLM to judge edge existence from title and abstract and trains a lightweight predictor on one LLM vector per node, because scoring all pairs is O(n²). Clean-accuracy figures in the excerpt are **88.38±1.06** on Cora and **80.03±2.16** on Citeseer. Those figures were not labeled as attack accuracy, and no Mettack, Nettack, or edge-injection protocol was seen in the sections read. No venue comment was on the Atom record ([Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15183)). **SDM-SCR** (accepted at ICME 2026, as stated in the arXiv comment) prompts an LLM to split node text into a task-relevant view and an irrelevant view, then regularizes only the signal view. No structural attack suite was seen. A table of “performance robustness” across LLMs is robustness to the choice of parser ([Li, Zhang, and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14746)). **ZeroGAR** benchmarks 13 zero-shot graph models under structural, textual, and node-injection attacks and was not read past the abstract opening, so it is unknown whether any of those models treats text as stable evidence ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33314)).

## Surveys, shift, and test time use a different toolbox

The phrase “increasing GNN robustness” covers more than edge attacks, and the surveys do not share one open-problem list. ## Conclusion

The 2024–2026 papers that were opened keep three designs in different rooms, and the attacks that move the numbers are aimed at the rule inside each room. Feature-residual reweighting (RUNG), a counted edge certificate (AuditVotes, AGNNCert), and a text-cosine gate (Guardual, the S&P 2026 purifier) can all be described loosely as “trust,” and the papers’ own evaluations show they answer different questions: an adaptive local PGD budget, a smoothing radius in number of edges, and a similarity threshold an attacker can refuse to cross. BadGraph’s result on RUNG is the warning at the joint. A method that up-weights edges because the endpoints look alike in a text embedding can be steered by an attacker who writes the text to look alike, and the drop reported under SBERT is over 50%. PGD-Guard, as configured in TGRB, is the same idea from the other side: only high-cosine pairs are eligible, so a cosine gate does not fire.

Zhang et al. already say that a defense rate on one attack does not travel, and they ask for something like a bound on prediction divergence that can be compared across architectures and attacks. That request is still open in the pages that were read, and it is why the headline accuracies above cannot be ranked against each other. A design that reweights from structural discrepancy during propagation, penalizes node-state movement under edge deletion, and spends a structural trust budget from frozen text scores is absent from the papers that were opened. The pieces that look similar stop at a feature residual, a filter certificate or a head penalty, or a hard text gate. GNNCert, RES-GRACE, the IJCAI 2026 test-time survey, Dai et al.’s robustness sub-taxonomy, and every abstract OpenAlex did not return were unread after HTTP 429 responses or missing abstracts, so those mechanisms stay unknown.
