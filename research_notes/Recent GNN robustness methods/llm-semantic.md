# LLM and text-semantic robustness on text-attributed graphs

Scope: papers from 2023–2026 (emphasis 2024–2026) that use an LLM, a prompted language model, or text embeddings to make graph learning more robust, or that attack that interface. Pure structural defenses and certified Lipschitz methods are out of scope. Today is 4 October 2026. Empirical numbers below are only those read in an arXiv abstract or HTML full text. Venue is recorded only when the arXiv comment or HTML stated it.

Paper roster used throughout:

- **LLM4RGNN** — “Can Large Language Models Improve the Adversarial Robustness of Graph Neural Networks?” Zhongjian Zhang, Xiao Wang, Huichi Zhou, Yue Yu, Mengmei Zhang, Cheng Yang, Chuan Shi. arXiv 2024-08 (2408.08685); comment: accepted by KDD 2025. Role: prompt-based malicious-edge judge, then two-stage distillation into a local LLM and an LM edge predictor that deletes malicious edges and adds missing ones. Teacher GPT-4 is not trained; the local LLM (Mistral-7B or Llama3-8B) is LoRA-tuned; the edge MLP is trained. Not a frozen pipeline. Evaluates topology attacks (Mettack, DICE, Nettack, adaptive, inductive poisoning). — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08685), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- **GraphEdit** — “GraphEdit: Large Language Models for Graph Structure Learning.” Zirui Guo, Lianghao Xia, Yanhua Yu, Yuling Wang, Kangkang Lu, Zhiyong Huang, Chao Huang. arXiv 2024-02-23, updated 2025-03-10 (2402.15183v5). No venue comment on the Atom record read. Role: instruction-tuned LLM edge-existence prompt (title/abstract → true/false and category) plus a lightweight edge predictor on LLM node vectors. The LLM is fine-tuned, not frozen. Setting read: denoising noisy or sparse structure on Cora, Citeseer, PubMed. Code: https://github.com/HKUDS/GraphEdit — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15183), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15183)
- **TGRB / SFT-auto / Guardual** — “Robustness in Text-Attributed Graph Learning: Insights, Trade-offs, and New Defenses.” HTML byline names Runlin Lei, Lu Yi, Mingguo He, Pengyu Qiu, and Zhewei Wei; a footnote also names Yongchao Liu. Full ordered author list was not confirmed (Atom fetch later returned HTTP 429). arXiv 2025-10-20 (2510.17185v1). No venue comment was recovered. Role: (i) benchmark of GNNs, robust GNNs, and GraphLLMs under text, structure, and hybrid poisoning and evasion; (ii) SFT-auto, a supervised-fine-tuned LLM classifier that keeps a node’s own text when structure looks attacked; (iii) Guardual, dual similarity thresholds on text embeddings (RoBERTa) extending GNNGuard-style filtering. SFT-auto fine-tunes the LLM. Code: https://github.com/Leirunlin/TGRB — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17185), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- **LLMEGNNRP** — “Are LLM-Enhanced Graph Neural Networks Robust against Poisoning Attacks?” Yuhang Ma, Jie Wang, Zheng Yan. arXiv 2026-03-27 (2603.26105); comment: to appear at IEEE S&P 2026. Role: LLM/LM feature enhancers (eight) in front of GCN/GAT/SAGE, plus a purification defense that drops an edge when cosine similarity of the LLM/LM embeddings is below a threshold. Freeze status of the enhancers was not stated in the sections read. Evaluates six structural poisoning attacks and three textual poisoning attacks (character, word, sentence) on Cora, Pubmed, Ogbn-products, and Tape-arxiv23. Code: https://github.com/CyberAlSec/LLMEGNNRP — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26105), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26105)
- **BadGraph** — “Can LLMs Fool Graph Learning? Exploring Universal Adversarial Attacks on Text-Attributed Graphs.” Zihui Chen, Yuling Wang, Pengfei Jiao, Kai Wu, Xiao Wang, Xiang Ao, Dalin Zhang. arXiv 2026-03-22 (2603.21155); comment: accepted by TheWebConf (WWW) 2026. Attack, not a defense. LLM jointly perturbs topology and node text. Victim side includes frozen-LLM reasoners as one paradigm the authors name. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21155), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)
- **GraphTextack** — “GRAPHTEXTACK: A Realistic Black-Box Node Injection Attack on LLM-Enhanced GNNs.” Jiaji Ma, Puja Trivedi, Danai Koutra. arXiv 2025-11-16 (2511.12423); comment: AAAI 2026. Attack. Injected nodes have crafted structure and semantics. The threat-model section says the text map ϕ may be a frozen LLM encoder, a fine-tuned model, or another text encoder. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12423), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12423)
- **SDM-SCR** — “Disentangle-then-Refine: LLM-Guided Decoupling and Structure-Aware Refinement for Graph Contrastive Learning.” Zhaoxing Li, Hai-Feng Zhang, Xiaoming Zhang. arXiv 2026-04-16 (2604.14746); comment: accept in ICME 2026. Role: prompted LLM parses node text into a task-relevant view and an irrelevant view; spectral consistency is then applied only on the signal view. No fine-tuning step was stated. This is attribute-noise disentanglement, not an edge-injection defense. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14746), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14746)
- **ZeroGAR** — “ZeroGAR: Benchmarking the Adversarial Robustness of Zero-Shot Graph Models.” Authors in the Atom snippet began Zhongjian Zhang, Xiao Wang, Busheng Zhang, Bo Yan, Xingtong Yu, Yue Gao, Jia Li, Chuan Shi; the print was capped at eight names, so the list may be longer. arXiv 2609.33314 (2026). No venue comment was in the snippet. Benchmark of 13 zero-shot graph models under structural, textual, and node-injection attacks. Not read beyond the abstract opening. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33314)

## Which 2024–2026 papers treat node text as evidence that stays reliable when the graph structure is attacked?

### Takeaway
Three 2025–2026 defenses explicitly use node text, or embeddings of that text, to decide which edges to trust after a structural attack: SFT-auto keeps the node’s own text and drops dissimilar neighbors, LLM4RGNN asks a language model whether an edge is malicious given the texts, and the IEEE S&P 2026 purification drops edges whose LLM/LM embeddings are dissimilar. Simply swapping in a stronger text encoder does not make the GNN robust: LLM4RGNN reports that six LLM/LM-enhanced GNNs still lose substantial accuracy under Mettack.

### Cited Findings
- LLM4RGNN’s own baseline check: six LLM/LM feature setups (OFA-Llama2-7B, OFA-SBert, TAPE, GCN-Llama-7B, GCN-e5-large, GCN-SBert) under Mettack at a 20% perturbation rate on Cora and PubMed suffer a maximum accuracy decrease of 37.9% and an average decrease of 23.1%. Vanilla GCN on the same check has a maximum decrease of 39.1% and an average of 35.5%. The authors read this as remaining vulnerability to topology attacks even after LLM feature enhancement. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- LLM4RGNN then uses node text as the evidence for structure repair. On a clean open-source graph they prompt GPT-4 for a maliciousness judgment and an analysis, build an instruction set, and fine-tune a local LLM to label malicious edges. Those labels train an LM edge predictor that also proposes missing edges. The repaired graph is the GNN input. The abstract states that in some cases, at a 40% perturbation ratio, GNN accuracy with LLM4RGNN is still better than accuracy on the clean graph. Attacks named in the section outline: Mettack, DICE, Nettack, adaptive attack, and inductive poisoning. They also report an “Impact of Text Quality” analysis; the numeric results of that section were not extracted. — [LLM4RGNN abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08685), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- GPT-4 instruction data size stated in the paper: maliciousness assessments and analyses of 26,518 edges. Implementation text also says about 26,518 instances, tuned with LoRA. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- SFT-auto (TGRB, Oct 2025) detects structure attacks without an edge-level LLM call: a node is flagged as structure-attacked if its embedding cosine similarity is below 0.5 to more than half of its neighbors. Recovery for those nodes “leverage[s] their preserved own text combined with filtered neighbors.” Connections to text-attacked nodes or low-similarity nodes are removed. If the text itself is flagged as attacked, the center text is discarded and the model uses neighbor text instead. So the paper’s rule is conditional: own text is the reliable evidence under structure attack, and it is not trusted under text attack. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- The same TGRB paper’s abstract finding (1): models have inherent robustness trade-offs between text and structure. Finding (2): GNN and robust-GNN performance depends heavily on the text encoder and the attack type. Finding (3): GraphLLMs are particularly vulnerable to training-data corruption. They introduce SFT-auto as one model aimed at both textual and structural attacks. Evaluation span stated in the abstract: classical GNNs, robust GNNs, and GraphLLMs, ten datasets, four domains, text-based, structure-based, and hybrid perturbations, poisoning and evasion. — [TGRB abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17185)
- TGRB reports that GNNGuard, a threshold filter originally not a 2024 method, becomes “top-tier” against inductive/evasion structural attacks once the node features are advanced text embeddings rather than BoW or TF-IDF, and that prior robust-GNN papers often evaluated only shallow embeddings. Guardual is their hyperparameter-free extension and “the leading RGNN in the structural evasion setting” in that paper’s claim. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- IEEE S&P 2026: LLM-enhanced GNNs “exhibit significantly higher accuracy and lower Relative Drop in Accuracy (RDA) than a shallow embedding-based baseline across various attack settings,” including structural poisoning. The authors attribute this partly to embeddings that encode structural and label information. No numeric RDA was copied from the sections read. Their purification defense computes cosine similarity of the LLM/LM embeddings of the two endpoints and removes edges below a threshold; on GCN under non-targeted structural attacks they call the defense “highly effective” relative to one input-level and one model-level defense they compare. Specific table numbers were not extracted. — [S&P 2026 abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26105), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26105)
- GraphEdit’s abstract says instruction-tuned LLMs over node text “effectively denoise noisy connections” and that experiments show “effectiveness and robustness” on benchmark graphs. The evaluation section read uses Cora (2,708 nodes, 5,429 edges), Citeseer (3,186 / 4,277), and PubMed (19,717 / 44,335). A clean-accuracy comparison table lists GraphEdit at 88.38 ± 1.06 (Cora) and 80.03 ± 2.16 (Citeseer) against GCN at 87.36 ± 1.60 and 78.87 ± 2.18. Those figures were not labeled as attack accuracy in the excerpt. No Mettack, Nettack, or edge-injection protocol was seen in the sections read. — [GraphEdit abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15183), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15183)
- SDM-SCR prompts an LLM to split raw node text into a relevant view and an irrelevant view, then applies semantic-consistency regularization only on the signal subspace. The abstract claims state-of-the-art accuracy and efficiency on TAG contrastive learning. A theoretical remark says the mechanism has a lower error bound than standard message passing under heterophily. No structural attack suite was seen. A table titled “performance robustness of SDM-SCR across different LLMs” compares Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-7B, Mistral-0.3-7B, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen-3-4B, and Gemma-3-1B; that is robustness to the choice of parser, not to an adversary. — [SDM-SCR html](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14746)

### Inferences
- The papers that match the “text stays reliable when structure is attacked” pattern are SFT-auto, LLM4RGNN, and the S&P 2026 embedding purification. All three still assume the text (or a precomputed embedding of it) was not the thing the attacker rewrote.
- TGRB’s own trade-off finding blocks a stronger reading: text is not treated as unconditionally reliable. SFT-auto throws the center text away when it classifies the node as text-attacked.
- Feature-encoder papers (TAPE, OFA, e5, Sentence-BERT) appear in this literature mainly as victims or as the embedding that a structural filter consumes. LLM4RGNN’s 23.1% average drop is direct evidence that a better text feature, by itself, is not a topology defense.

### Gaps
- Atom metadata for TGRB’s full author list and any venue was not retrieved after HTTP 429. Yongchao Liu is named in a footnote in the HTML extract; whether other Ant Group names on the scrambled byline are coauthors was not resolved.
- GraphEdit’s “robustness” experiments were not confirmed to be adversarial edge injection. Only noise/sparsity structure learning and clean accuracy figures were read.
- SDM-SCR’s heterophily statement is a proof claim in the paper; the attack model (if any) behind that bound was not read.
- ZeroGAR’s abstract says zero-shot graph models are tested under textual as well as structural and node-injection attacks, but the body was not read, so it is not known whether any ZeroGAR model treats text as stable evidence.
- A 2026 privacy paper, “Are LLM-Enhanced GNNs Privacy-Safe?” (arXiv 2608.25727, Longzhu He, Zelang Wen, Chaozhuo Li, Sen Su), studies link, label, and membership inference on TAG embeddings. It is outside this adversarial-robustness slice and was not used as evidence here. — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25727)

## Do any papers allocate a structural trust budget using semantic scores, or multiply a structural weight by a text similarity?

### Takeaway
No paper in the set that was read multiplies a structural edge weight or attention coefficient by a text similarity, and none uses the phrase “trust budget.” The closest mechanisms are hard gates: drop or keep an edge if an LLM maliciousness score or a text-embedding cosine crosses a threshold. Guardual’s 0.7/0.3 weights choose the threshold, they are not edge multipliers.

### Cited Findings
- IEEE S&P 2026 purification: cosine similarity of LLM/LM embeddings of the two endpoints; remove the edge if similarity is below a predefined threshold. The embeddings are the semantic score. The operation described is deletion, not a product with an existing structural weight. — [S&P 2026 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26105)
- Guardual (TGRB appendix F.3) replaces GNNGuard’s single validation-tuned threshold with two similarity thresholds on a given embedding. The paper states the listed thresholds are for RoBERTa embeddings. Training uses a conservative threshold chosen by score = 0.7 · P_preserve + 0.3 · P_filter, where P_preserve is the fraction of intra-class edges retained and P_filter is the fraction of inter-class edges removed. Test uses score = 0.5 · P_preserve + 0.5 · P_filter. Table 12 training/test thresholds include Cora 0.250/0.503, CiteSeer 0.320/0.580, PubMed 0.360/0.633, WikiCS 0.283/0.433, Instagram 0.000/0.471, Reddit 0.000/0.177, History 0.260/0.457, Photo 0.000/0.476, Computer 0.000/0.457, ArXiv 0.320/0.540. The paper says Guardual improves on GNNGuard, with the largest gains on Computer and Photo, “+8.23% and +10.74% respectively.” — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- TGRB describes original GNNGuard, in this TAG re-evaluation, as “threshold-based filtering,” and says the gain comes from “dataset- and embedding-specific filtering” of text embeddings. The excerpt does not write an updated edge weight of the form A_ij · cos(text_i, text_j). — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- SFT-auto’s structural test is also a gate: cosine below 0.5 to more than half the neighbors flags the node; low-similarity connections are removed. There is no continuous reweighting formula in the excerpt. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- LLM4RGNN turns GPT-4/local-LLM maliciousness into discrete decisions. If no edges would be removed, the purification threshold β is chosen from {1, 2}; otherwise from {2, 3, 4}. The LM edge predictor adds an edge when its probability exceeds γ, tuned in {0.91, 0.93, 0.95, 0.97, 0.99}. Cosine similarity is used to build a negative candidate pool (the 4,000 lowest-similarity node pairs), not as a multiplier on the adjacency. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- TGRB’s attack budget is an attack constraint, not a trust budget: ∥A′ − A∥_0 counts edge edits and a sum of indicators counts modified node texts. Default structural budget in the PGD setup they describe is 20% of edges. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- BadGraph’s discussion of RUNG (Hou et al., 2024) says RUNG “employs edge reweighting to prune suspicious edges,” and that BadGraph’s semantically aligned perturbations cause RUNG to “overemphasize adversarial edges.” The excerpt does not say RUNG’s weight is a text similarity. Under SBERT encoding, BadGraph reduces RUNG’s accuracy by over 50%. — [BadGraph html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)

### Inferences
- Semantic scores in this slice are used as delete/keep tests (cosine threshold or LLM maliciousness bin), which is a crude trust allocation: low score spends none of the edge, high score keeps it.
- A product “structural weight × text similarity” was not found. Guardual’s 0.7 and 0.3 are weights on a threshold-selection objective.
- An attacker who only adds high-similarity edges (see the next section’s PGD-Guard setup) is aiming at exactly these gates.

### Gaps
- The original GNNGuard formula (Zhang and Zitnik, 2020) was not re-read. TGRB may inherit a cosine edge coefficient and only retune the threshold; that formula was not on the pages extracted, so it is not claimed here.
- RUNG’s reweighting formula was not opened. It is cited as a robust-GNN baseline that BadGraph misleads, not as a text-semantic method confirmed in this pass.
- No paper was found that states a numeric “trust budget” (for example, keep the top-k semantic edges per node, or a KL budget on edge mass). Absence is limited to the queries and papers actually opened.

## How do they avoid calling an LLM on every edge at training time?

### Takeaway
The methods that would otherwise be quadratic do not prompt an LLM on every pair during GNN training. They either call the LLM once per node and cache the vector, distill edge judgments into a small MLP, or restrict the LLM to a sampled or top-k candidate set. BM25 was not used in any method section that was read. Sentence embeddings (Sentence-BERT, e5, RoBERTa) are the usual cached substitute for an LLM edge call.

### Cited Findings
- LLM4RGNN, distillation: GPT-4 is queried to build the instruction set (26,518 edges from a clean graph), not to score the attacked graph at training time. That set fine-tunes a local LLM. The local LLM’s edge labels are then distilled into an MLP on concatenated LM features, ŷ_e(v_i, v_j) = MLP(h_i ∥ h_j), trained with cross-entropy. At use time the local LLM identifies malicious edges; the MLP proposes missing edges. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- LLM4RGNN, caching and candidate restriction, stated as the large-graph extension: cache edges already inferred by the local LLM, and run the O(|V|^2) predictor only on the top-K most similar nodes per node, K ≪ |V|, reducing that step to O(K|V|). They point at vLLM for parallel local-LLM inference. They report this on full OGBN-Arxiv (169,343 nodes, 1,166,243 edges). A separate efficiency note says each experiment’s total inference time is kept within 90 minutes; that sentence sits next to the large-graph discussion and was not tied to a single dataset in the excerpt. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685)
- GraphEdit states that running the instruction-tuned LLM on all pairs is O(n^2) and impractical. Their lightweight edge predictor is trained on previously sampled node pairs and scores candidates using one vector per node, h_i = LLM(s_i), h_j = LLM(s_j), so the LLM encodes nodes rather than edges. Instruction tuning itself uses node pairs drawn uniformly from the training nodes. The LLM is still invoked for refinement of selected candidates; the excerpt does not say the LLM is skipped entirely after the predictor exists. — [GraphEdit html](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15183)
- S&P 2026 purification never calls an LLM per edge. Cosine is computed on embeddings the feature enhancer already produced for nodes. — [S&P 2026 html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26105)
- Guardual precomputes scores from the chosen text embedding (RoBERTa in Table 12) and stores two thresholds per dataset. No per-edge LLM call is described. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- SFT-auto’s cost note: both SFT-auto and SFT-neighbor are bottlenecked by one LLM forward pass per sample, not per edge. Training uses at most 1.3× more samples because of augmentation with ratio r ≤ 0.15. Structure detection is embedding cosine, not an LLM edge prompt. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- SDM-SCR’s LLM is a per-node text parser (relevant vs irrelevant view under a task prompt). No edge-wise prompting was described. — [SDM-SCR html](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14746)
- Sentence-embedding encoders that show up as the cached text features, not as edge LLMs: Sentence-BERT and e5-large in LLM4RGNN’s six-encoder baseline; RoBERTa as Guardual’s embedding and as the target embedding of the PGD-Guard attack in TGRB; SBERT and TAPE as BadGraph victim encoders. — [LLM4RGNN html](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08685), [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185), [BadGraph html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)
- Attack-side analogue, not a training defense: BadGraph says black-box fake-edge search would query all node pairs with an LLM at O(V^2). Their target-influencer retrieval narrows prompting to a small influencer set instead of every pair. — [BadGraph html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)

### Inferences
- “Distill, then cache node vectors, then score only candidates” is the repeated pattern (LLM4RGNN and GraphEdit). Threshold defenses skip the LLM edge call altogether once node embeddings exist.
- BM25 is not part of this pattern in the sources opened. The only IDF-related hit in LLM4RGNN was a bibliography entry for Robertson’s IDF paper next to the Sentence-BERT citation, which is not a method claim.

### Gaps
- It was not read whether GraphEdit’s candidate predictor is a trained MLP, a similarity on h_i, or both. The excerpt gives h_i = LLM(s_i) and says those vectors train the predictor, then stops.
- Whether the S&P feature enhancers are frozen off-the-shelf encoders or fine-tuned was not stated in the extracted sections. GraphTextack only says ϕ “may” be frozen.
- No BM25, sparse lexical index, or inverted-index edge filter was found in the method sections read. That is a negative from this pass, not a proof none exists.

## Which threat models are text-camouflaged edges, where injected edges are chosen to look lexically plausible?

### Takeaway
The closest 2024–2026 threat models make injections look semantically or lexically close, but they do it in different places. PGD-Guard, as configured in TGRB, only flips edges whose RoBERTa cosine is already high. BadGraph rewrites node text under an explicit lexical-overlap budget so the text stays fluent, and pairs that with topology edits that preserve homophily. Neither paper was seen selecting injected edges by lexical overlap or BM25. GraphTextack and word-level text injection attacks craft the text of injected nodes rather than ranking edges by lexical plausibility.

### Cited Findings
- TGRB specifies PGD-Guard (citing Mujkanovic et al., 2022) as an adaptive structural attack that only perturbs node pairs whose cosine similarity exceeds a threshold, “emulating defense-aware strategies intended to bypass similarity filtering (targeting GNNGuard).” In their configuration the cosine thresholds searched are {0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7}, the budget is 20% of edges, and the target embedding is RoBERTa. That is embedding-camouflaged edge edits: the injected or flipped edge is constrained to look similar in text-embedding space. It is not described as a lexical or BM25 constraint. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- BadGraph jointly perturbs adjacency and node text. The text rewrite is constrained to “retain partial lexical content” for fluency and stealth, and to insert an influencer keyword so the text drifts toward the influencer’s category. The formal constraint is an L0 budget ∥S′_i − S_i∥_0 ≤ b_{S_i}. A separate homophily-preserving bound is used to argue that sparse joint edits stay stealthy. The attack is reported to cut accuracy by up to 76.3% across GNN and LLM reasoners, and by over 50% on RUNG under SBERT, which the authors attribute to semantically aligned edges being up-weighted. Influencer retrieval is what chooses where to attach the shortcut; the excerpt does not say edges are ranked by lexical similarity. — [BadGraph abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21155), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.21155)
- GraphTextack (AAAI 2026) is a black-box poisoning node-injection attack. Injected nodes get both connectivity and text features, searched by an evolutionary algorithm whose fitness balances local prediction change and global influence. The abstract says uni-modal structure-only or text-only attacks cause only modest degradation on LLM-enhanced GNNs, and that GraphTextack “significantly outperforms 12 strong baselines” on five datasets and two LLM-enhanced GNN targets. No lexical-plausibility edge ranker was described in the sections read. The paper does say LLM-derived features are vulnerable to prompt injection and adversarial phrasing. — [GraphTextack abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12423), [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12423)
- TGRB’s hybrid text-level injection setup, which they label WTGIA and cite as “Word-level Text GIA (Lei et al., 2024),” generates injected-node text with Llama-3.1-8B, no-topic prompts, and vocabulary masking, then adds edges. At perturbation rate 0.2 they state injected-edge counts of 17 (Cora), 9 (CiteSeer), and 22 (PubMed), and injected-node counts of 60, 90, and 400. This is camouflage of the injected node’s text by a language model, plus a separate edge budget. The excerpt does not say those edges are chosen to be lexically similar to existing edges. The Llama-3.1 configuration is TGRB’s reproduction setting, not a quote from the 2024 paper. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- The 2024 text-level GIA source itself was only partly identified. An arXiv Atom entry dated 2024-05-26, comment “Accepted by NeurIPS 2024,” authors Runlin Lei, Yuwei Hu, Yuchen Ren, and Zhewei Wei, ends its abstract by saying further research is needed on the practical significance of text-level GIAs. The title and arXiv id were in a truncated API response and a retry returned HTTP 429, so they are not recorded here. TGRB is the source for the “word-level” label. — first Atom search on 4 Oct 2026; citation string in [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)
- S&P 2026 says they “propose a new combined attack” that joins structural and textual poisoning, and a purification defense against it. The combined attack’s selection rule (whether injected edges must look lexically plausible) was not in the extracted paragraphs. Textual attacks they do specify, as an evaluation axis, operate at character, word, and sentence levels. — [S&P 2026 abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26105)
- TGRB explicitly de-emphasizes some lexical attacks: they “do not use gradient-based or unnoticeable word-level textual attacks” in the main structural/text comparison because those attacks “have been shown to have poor transferability.” That is a statement about word-level edits of existing node text, not about camouflaged edge insertion. — [TGRB html](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17185)

### Inferences
- “Text-camouflaged edges” in the strict sense (choose the injected edge because the two texts look lexically alike) was not found. The operational cousin that was found is embedding-camouflaged edges: PGD-Guard refuses low RoBERTa-cosine pairs so a cosine gate will not drop the edit.
- BadGraph camouflages the text of a node (keep some original tokens, drift the meaning) and separately edits topology under a homophily argument. The harm to edge reweighting is a consequence they measure, not a lexical edge-ranking algorithm.
- Node-injection attacks (GraphTextack, WTGIA as reproduced in TGRB) camouflage the new node’s text with an LLM or an evolutionary text search, then attach edges under a budget. Edge choice and lexical plausibility are separate in the descriptions that were read.

### Gaps
- Title, arXiv id, and method details of Lei, Hu, Ren, and Wei, NeurIPS 2024, on text-level graph injection were not recovered. Whether that paper picks edges by lexical similarity is unknown here.
