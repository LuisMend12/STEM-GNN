## How do 2024–2026 papers connect a Lipschitz constraint to message-passing dynamics, rather than only to the last linear layer?

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 papers that actually constrain or bound a Lipschitz quantity on the propagator do it in the filter or the aggregation, not in a final linear layer: Chebyshev and attention Lipschitz constants composed through a gate (SpecSphere), bi-Lipschitz message-passing embeddings with respect to Weisfeiler–Leman graph metrics (FSW-GNN, building on SortMPNN), and spectral norms of the diffusion operator inside message-passing generalization bounds (PAC-Bayes). STEM-GNN is the contrasting case: the training penalty bounds the prediction head, while message passing appears as a low/high-frequency witness rather than as a constrained encoder Lipschitz constant.

### Cited Findings
- SpecSphere (full title: Adaptive Branch Specialization in Spectral-Spatial Graph Neural Networks for Certified Robustness; Yoonhyuk Choi, Jiho Choi, Chong-Kwon Kim; arXiv 13 May 2025, HTML of v3 dated 1 Aug 2025; venue not stated on the pages read). The certified object is the infinity-norm change of the fused node representation under a joint threat set \(\mathcal{S}(G;p,\varepsilon)\) with \(\|\Delta A\|_2\le\sqrt{2p}\) and an \(\ell_\infty\) feature perturbation. Branch-wise Lipschitz bounds are derived for the propagators themselves. Spectral branch: rescaled Laplacian in \([-1,1]\), Chebyshev factors satisfy \(\|T_k(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})\|_2\le 1\) and \(\|T_k(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}+\Delta)-T_k(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})\|_2\le k\,2^{k-1}\|\Delta\|_2\), then \(B_{\mathrm{spec}}^{A}:=(2^{K+1}-1)\|\mathbf{c}\|_1\|X\|_\infty\) and \(B_{\mathrm{spec}}^{X}:=(2^{K+1}-1)\|\mathbf{c}\|_1\). Spatial branch: each attention layer is assumed to have weight spectral norm \(\le\beta\) and \(\|\partial\hat{A}/\partial A\|_2\le 1\), giving \(B_{\mathrm{spat}}^{A}:=\beta^{L_p}\|X\|_\infty\) and \(B_{\mathrm{spat}}^{X}:=\beta^{L_p}\). Gate: MLP weight spectral norms \(\|W_\ell\|_2\le\gamma_\ell\), sigmoid derivative \(\le 1/4\), so \(L_{\mathrm{gate}}:=\frac14 L_\varphi(B_{\mathrm{spec}}^{A}+B_{\mathrm{spat}}^{A})\) with respect to \(A\) and the analogous constant with respect to \(X\). Theorem 4 composes these through \(Z=\alpha\odot Z_{\mathrm{spec}}+(1-\alpha)\odot Z_{\mathrm{spat}}\) and couples them to margins. Training described on the page is branch-specific adversarial inner maximization (structure versus feature), low-pass and high-pass regularizers, and a consistency penalty, not a statement that spectral normalization is applied. — [arXiv:2505.08320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)
- FSW-GNN (full title: FSW-GNN: A Bi-Lipschitz WL-Equivalent Graph Neural Network; Yonatan Sverdlov, Yair Davidson, Nadav Dym, Tal Amir; arXiv 10 Oct 2024; accepted at the Fourth Learning on Graphs Conference, LoG 2025). Quantity: bi-Lipschitz constant of the message-passing embedding with respect to the doubly stochastic metric and the Tree Mover’s Distance, both described as WL-equivalent graph metrics. Enforcement: architecture (Fourier sliced-Wasserstein aggregation), not a penalty and not a randomized-smoothing radius. The page states that standard MPNNs are not lower-Lipschitz, which is why WL-separable graphs can land on nearly identical embeddings, and that upper Lipschitzness of MPNNs had been discussed earlier while bi-Lipschitzness was an open goal. Scope stated on the page: graphs of bounded size with continuous, bounded node features; the text says the results readily extend to edge features. This is separation stability (oversmoothing and oversquashing), not an adversarial \(\ell_2\) or edge-edit certificate. — [arXiv:2410.09118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09118)
- On the Hölder Stability of Multiset and Graph Neural Networks (Yair Davidson, Nadav Dym; arXiv 11 Jun 2024; venue not stated on the arXiv record that was read). Quantity: uniform upper Lipschitzness of the parametric MPNNs they consider, plus a lower Hölder-in-expectation exponent for sum aggregation; SortMPNN is lower-Lipschitz in expectation (they call it the first MPNN with both upper and lower Lipschitz guarantees under that weaker expectation notion). Enforcement: architecture (sort-based multiset aggregation, and an adaptive-bias ReLU multiset map). They state that smooth multiset activations can be injective yet not lower-Lipschitz, that the upper Lipschitzness of GIN was already shown by Chuang and Jegelka (2022), and that sum-based MPNNs fail a binary task on graph pairs that three 1-WL iterations can separate. FSW-GNN later says its proof also shows SortMPNN is bi-Lipschitz in the standard sense, and that SortMPNN pads neighborhoods to a fixed maximum size and does not support non-integer edge weights. — [arXiv:2406.06984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06984); padding limitation — [arXiv:2410.09118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09118)
- PAC-Bayesian Adversarially Robust Generalization Bounds for Graph Neural Network (Tan Sun, Junhong Lin; arXiv 6 Feb 2024, v2; venue not stated). Quantity: a robust generalization gap, not a per-node certified radius. The page says the gap is governed by the spectral norm of the feature diffusion matrix \(\|P_G\|_2\), the spectral and Frobenius norms of the weights, and a perturbation intensity \(\varepsilon\). Message passing enters because \(\|P_G\|_2\) replaces a maximum-degree factor; for a normalized Laplacian they note the spectral norm is less than one, and for the adjacency matrix they recover \(\|A\|_2\le d\). Assumptions stated: node features in a spectral-norm ball \(\|X\|_2\le B\); message, update, and readout maps are \(L\)-Lipschitz and centered at zero. They write that the bounds “do not have a significant effect on the graph edge attack,” and that one could overlook edge attacks and focus on optimization error from those attacks. Limitations they state: graph classification only, because node-classification samples are not independent; the optimization algorithm is not modeled. — [arXiv:2402.04038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04038)
- PAC-Bayesian Adversarially Robust Generalization for Message Passing Graph Neural Networks: A Sensitivity Analysis (Ziling Liang, Xinping Yi, Qingsong Wen, Shi Jin; arXiv 4 Jun 2026; venue not stated). Quantity: a tighter robust PAC-Bayesian margin bound for message-passing GNNs. They derive output Jacobians with respect to weight blocks (input-injection weights and message-passing weights separately, because MPGNNs are “non-homogeneous”), use anisotropic Gaussian posteriors, and state that the leading dimension factor drops from hidden width to the number of classes \(K\). The introduction says local adversarial perturbations are amplified by message passing. Formal setup that was visible: \(\|X\|_{2,\infty}\le B\), elementwise maps \(L\)-Lipschitz and centered, and each graph replaced by a worst admissible perturbation. The exact admissible set (feature ball versus edge edits) was not fully visible in the extracted HTML, so that set is unverified beyond the introduction’s mention of feature or structure perturbations. This is an analysis bound, not a training constraint. — [arXiv:2606.06293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06293)
- STEM-GNN (full title: Generalizing GNNs with Tokenized Mixture of Experts; Xiaoguang Guo, Zehong Wang, Jiazheng Li, Shawn Spitzel, Qi Yang, Kaize Ding, Jundong Li, Chuxu Zhang; arXiv 9 Feb 2026; comment: accepted to KDD 2026). The enforced Lipschitz quantity is the prediction head: “A Frobenius penalty on the prediction head bounds its Lipschitz constant,” cited to Miyato et al. (2018), and the stability constraint is written \(L_h\eta_\theta\rho\le\varepsilon\). Message passing is in the theory as graph filters \(P_L(G)\) and \(P_H(G)\) that split a low-frequency and a high-frequency component; Theorem 3.1 says a Lipschitz stability constraint caps reliance on the high component and leaves a worst-environment floor. The admissible set is defined with both a feature budget \(\rho_x\) and a structure budget \(\rho_s\). This is a stability/generalization argument under frozen deployment, not a certificate that a prediction is constant inside a radius. The arXiv API abstract says “nine” benchmarks; the HTML introduction says “eight.” Both wordings were on the pages read. — [arXiv:2602.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09258)
- mHC-GNN (full title: mHC-GNN: Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections for Graph Neural Networks; Subhankar Mishra; arXiv 5 Jan 2026; venue not stated). Not an adversarial certificate. The architectural constraint is Sinkhorn–Knopp projection of stream-mixing matrices onto doubly stochastic matrices. Theorem 5.2 bounds node-pair differences: a residual contraction \((1-\varepsilon)^L\), compared with the usual spectral-gap rate \((1-\gamma)^L\), and an orthogonal-complement bound \((1-2\varepsilon)^L\) while depth is below the stream count. The mixing is applied before neighborhood aggregation so the subspace argument survives sparse message passing. The page says orthogonal matrices preserve norms but not means, which is why they use doubly stochastic matrices instead. Ablation numbers on the page: No-Sinkhorn gives Chameleon \(18.20\pm 0.00\), Texas \(10.81\pm 0.00\), Cora Full \(13.00\pm 0.00\), described as deterministic collapse, “up to 82% degradation.” — [arXiv:2601.02451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02451)

### Inferences
- “Lipschitz of message passing” in this window means three different objects: a Lipschitz constant of a polynomial filter or attention stack with respect to \(\Delta A\) and \(\Delta X\) (SpecSphere), a bi-Lipschitz embedding of graphs under a WL metric (FSW-GNN / SortMPNN), and a spectral norm of the diffusion matrix inside a generalization bound (Sun and Lin; Liang et al.). Only SpecSphere turns that constant into a prediction certificate.
- A Frobenius or spectral penalty on the last readout, as in STEM-GNN, does not by itself Lipschitz-constrain the aggregator. The papers that do constrain aggregation use either an architectural aggregator (sort, sliced Wasserstein, doubly stochastic stream mix, Chebyshev factor bounds) or an assumption that layer spectral norms stay \(\le\beta\).

### Gaps
- Whether SpecSphere enforces \(\|W\|_2\le\beta\) by spectral normalization, by projection, or only as a hypothesis of the proof was not visible in the extracted sections.
- No opened 2024–2026 paper states a certified radius that is the Lipschitz constant of a GraphSAGE or GCN message function under an \(\ell_2\) feature ball alone.
- GNNCert’s own Lipschitz or smoothing argument was not read (arXiv rate limit).

## Which papers certify robustness to edge edits versus feature L2 or feature-dropout perturbations?

### Takeaway
Edge edits and node injection are the certified threat models in the 2024–2026 defenses that were opened: randomized smoothing radii in number of edges (AuditVotes), a collective certificate for injected nodes and their edges, and deterministic certified perturbation sizes that count edges, nodes, and arbitrarily rewritten feature vectors together (AGNNCert, PGNNCert). Feature \(\ell_2\) balls were not the certified object in those defenses. SpecSphere certifies \(\ell_0\) edge flips jointly with \(\ell_\infty\) feature perturbations. Whole-node feature ablation appears as smoothing noise in message-interception certificates, whose attacker may arbitrarily rewrite features of a bounded number of nodes.

### Cited Findings
- AuditVotes (full title: AuditVotes: Elevating Provable Defense for GNNs with Efficient Augmentation and Conditional Smoothing; Yuni Lai, Yulin Zhu, Yixuan Sun, Yulun Wu, Bin Xiao, Gaolei Li, Jianhua Li, Qi Xie, Kai Zhou; arXiv 29 Mar 2025, v3 updated 22 Jul 2026; comment: accepted by CCS 2026). Quantity: randomized-smoothing certified radius for graph edits, evaluated as certified accuracy under an edge-attack budget. Enforcement: majority vote over randomized graphs, plus graph-rewiring augmentation (Jaccard, a feature autoencoder, or multi-head feature similarity) and a confidence filter that drops low-confidence votes. They prove the certificate survives an arbitrary filter if the certification procedure is adapted. Threat model in the experiments that were visible: edge insertion/deletion (inductive node classification). They also say the plug-in applies to de-randomized graph smoothing and to Gaussian image smoothing. Numbers on the page: on Cora-ML under 20-edge attacks, clean accuracy improves by 437.1% and certified accuracy by 409.3% relative to their vanilla-smoothing baseline; on Citeseer, edge-insertion attacks up to 20 edges, SimAug moves clean accuracy from 14.7% to 70.9%, and SimAug+Conf moves certified accuracy from 14.7% to 72.6%, with runtime increase stated as under 1%; robust accuracy 83.3% on Cora-ML at 5 edges per node; applied to GNNCert (Xia et al., 2024), SimAug improves clean accuracy by 12.7% and certified accuracy by 13.9% on Cora-ML. Those percents are the paper’s reported relative or absolute changes, not radii. — [arXiv:2503.22998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22998)
- Collective Certified Robustness against Graph Injection Attacks (Yuni Lai, Bailin Pan, Kaihuang Chen, Yancheng Yuan, Kai Zhou; arXiv 3 Mar 2024; venue not stated). Quantity: a collective certified ratio, a lower bound on the fraction of a target set whose predictions stay fixed under one shared perturbed graph. Enforcement: randomized smoothing via node-aware bi-smoothing as they attribute it to Lai et al. (2023) — each edge deleted with probability \(p_e\), each node deleted with probability \(p_n\) — then a binary integer quadratic constrained linear program relaxed to an LP. Threat: evasion graph injection. Injected nodes have arbitrary features; each injected node adds at most \(\tau\) edges. This is not an \(\ell_2\) feature ball on existing nodes and not a pure edge-flip model. Number on the page: on Citeseer, certified ratio from 0.0% to 81.2% when injected nodes are 5% of the graph, LP solved within 1 minute; they also say the ratio goes from 0.0% to over 80.0% on both Cora-ML and Citeseer at that budget. They say sample-wise certificates assume a different perturbed graph per node and a fixed receptive field, while injection adds edges and expands the receptive field. — [arXiv:2403.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01423)
- AGNNCert (full title: AGNNCert: Defending Graph Neural Networks against Arbitrary Perturbations with Deterministic Certification; Jiate Li, Binghui Wang; arXiv 2 Feb 2025; comment: accepted by USENIX Security 2025). Quantity: a deterministic certified perturbation size, a threshold on the total number of manipulated nodes, nodes whose features are manipulated, and edges. Enforcement: hash a graph into edge-disjoint subgraphs (edge-centric) or into directed subgraphs keyed by outgoing endpoints (node-centric), train or evaluate a base GNN per subgraph, majority vote. They state one edge edit changes one subgraph prediction; node-centric division makes the node or feature term equal to the number of injected, deleted, or feature-rewritten nodes, including a node with arbitrarily many incident edges. Threat: test-time evasion, simultaneous edge insertion and deletion, node insertion and deletion, and arbitrary node-feature changes. Not an \(\ell_2\) or \(\ell_\infty\) radius; a feature change is counted per node, with the feature rewrite unrestricted inside that count. They say prior certificates restrict the attacker to one perturbation type, except GNNCert (their citation: Xia et al., 2024) which covers edge and/or node-feature perturbation, and that every method except one has a probabilistic rather than deterministic guarantee. — [arXiv:2502.00765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00765)
- PGNNCert (full title: Deterministic Certification of Graph Neural Networks against Graph Poisoning Attacks with Arbitrary Perturbations; Jiate Li, Meng Pang, Yun Dong, Binghui Wang; arXiv 24 Mar 2025; comment: accepted at CVPR 2025). Same certified object and the same edge-centric / node-centric hash-and-vote construction, applied to training-time poisoning rather than test-time evasion. Threat: arbitrary combined edits of training edges, nodes, and node features; white-box access to the training graphs. The guarantee is that the test prediction is unchanged if the poisoning size stays within the certified perturbation size. They say earlier provable defenses are limited to one perturbation type, often one architecture or task, and are probabilistic. No certified-accuracy percentage was copied from the HTML that was read. — [arXiv:2503.18503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18503)
- SpecSphere, same bibliographic record as above. Joint certificate: \(\ell_0\) edge flips (through \(\|\Delta A\|_2\le\sqrt{2p}\)) and \(\ell_\infty\) feature perturbations. Both, not feature \(\ell_2\). — [arXiv:2505.08320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)
- Randomized Message-Interception Smoothing: Gray-box Certificates for Graph Neural Networks (Yan Scholten, Jan Schuchardt, Simon Geisler, Aleksandar Bojchevski, Stephan Günnemann; arXiv 5 Jan 2023, v2; comment: accepted at NeurIPS 2022; HTML header showed a 10 Nov 2024 revision stamp). Included because later certificates cite it and the arXiv text was revised inside the window; the venue year is 2022. Quantity: a probabilistic certificate that the majority vote is constant when an attacker controls at most \(\rho\) nodes. Enforcement: smooth by randomly deleting edges and/or ablating (masking) all features of random nodes, then bound the probability that a message from a controlled node reaches the target. Threat they state: evasion feature perturbations; the attacker arbitrarily rewrites features of \(\rho\) whole nodes. The edge deletions and feature masks are the defender’s noise, not the attack. They explicitly contrast this with black-box smoothing. Number on the page: certificates on Cora-ML in 17 seconds, versus up to 25 minutes for Bojchevski et al. (2020). No certified-accuracy percentage was copied. — [arXiv:2301.02039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02039)
- Sun and Lin (2024), same record as above. The robust generalization theorem that was visible is about feature perturbations inside a spectral-norm ball plus intensity \(\varepsilon\). The authors say the bound does not meaningfully cover edge attacks. This is not a certified prediction radius. — [arXiv:2402.04038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04038)

### Inferences
- Discrete combinatorial certificates (edge budget, injection budget, or a counted sum of edges, nodes, and rewritten feature vectors) are what 2024–2026 GNN certification papers in this sample actually prove. Continuous feature \(\ell_2\) certification of the Cohen–Salman–Kolter type was not found for graphs in the opened papers; AuditVotes mentions Gaussian smoothing only for images.
- “Feature dropout” as a certificate shows up as node-feature ablation inside message-interception smoothing, and as a per-node arbitrary feature rewrite inside AGNNCert and PGNNCert. Those are not the same guarantee: ablation is noise the smoother adds, while AGNNCert counts an unrestricted feature replacement as one unit of budget.

### Gaps
- Primary texts of GNNCert (Xia et al. 2024) and of de-randomized smoothers cited beside it (Yang et al. 2024; the Li and Wang 2025 citation in AuditVotes) were not opened. AuditVotes says those are de-randomized graph smoothing schemes and reports the Cora-ML plug-in numbers above. Identifying Li and Wang 2025 with AGNNCert is an inference from author names and year, not a citation that was seen.
- No opened paper reported a certified \(\ell_2\) feature radius for a GNN. Absence from this sample is not a proof that none exists; the second arXiv query batch failed with HTTP 429.
- The exact attack model inside Liang et al. (2026) beyond “worst admissible perturbation” plus the introduction’s feature-or-structure sentence was not extracted.

## What training penalties or architectural constraints are used?

### Takeaway
In the opened 2024–2026 defenses, the mechanisms that are actually used are randomized smoothing (edge and node deletion noise, plus AuditVotes’ rewiring and confidence filter), deterministic hash partitions with majority vote, Chebyshev polynomial filters whose operator Lipschitz constants are proved, sort or sliced-Wasserstein aggregators for bi-Lipschitz embeddings, Sinkhorn doubly stochastic mixing, adversarial inner maximization with frequency and consistency penalties, and a Frobenius penalty on a prediction head. Orthogonal weight constraints and spectral normalization of GNN layers were not found as training methods for certificates in this sample. Spectral norms appear as assumptions or as terms in generalization bounds.

### Cited Findings
- Randomized smoothing. AuditVotes adds noise by the host smoothing scheme (they discuss SparseSmooth’s edge-addition probability \(p_+\) from Bojchevski et al. 2020 as the baseline that must raise \(p_+\) to enlarge the radius), then rewires each randomized graph from features and drops low-confidence votes. Collective injection certificates use edge-deletion and node-deletion smoothing. Message-interception smoothing uses edge deletion and whole-node feature ablation. — [arXiv:2503.22998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22998); [arXiv:2403.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01423); [arXiv:2301.02039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02039)
- Deterministic partition constraints. AGNNCert and PGNNCert do not penalize a weight norm. They constrain the computation graph: a hash puts each edge, or each node’s outgoing edges, into one subgraph so a single edit changes a bounded number of votes. — [arXiv:2502.00765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00765); [arXiv:2503.18503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18503)
- Polynomial propagation. SpecSphere’s spectral branch is a Chebyshev expansion of order \(K\) on the normalized Laplacian. The certificate uses \(\|T_k\|_2\le 1\) and the difference bound \(k\,2^{k-1}\|\Delta\|_2\), then \((2^{K+1}-1)\|\mathbf{c}\|_1\). The spatial branch is attention-style message passing under a spectral-norm assumption \(\beta\), not a polynomial. Training penalties named on the page: branch-specific adversarial loss, \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LP}}\) and \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{HP}}\), and consistency weight \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}\). A heterophilic setting they describe uses \(\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}=1.0\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}=10^{-1}\); they say \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}<10^{-3}\) fails to align branches and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cons}}\ge 1.0\) blocks adaptive fusion. — [arXiv:2505.08320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)
- Bi-Lipschitz aggregators. FSW-GNN replaces standard sum aggregation by a Fourier sliced-Wasserstein map so the MPNN is bi-Lipschitz for the doubly stochastic metric and Tree Mover’s Distance. SortMPNN uses coordinate sorting of neighborhoods. Neither paper describes an orthogonal-weight penalty. — [arXiv:2410.09118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09118); [arXiv:2406.06984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06984)
- Doubly stochastic architectural constraint. mHC-GNN projects stream-mixing matrices onto the Birkhoff polytope with Sinkhorn–Knopp. The page distinguishes this from orthogonal mixing. — [arXiv:2601.02451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02451)
- Head penalty only. STEM-GNN’s equation (22), as described in the HTML, is a Frobenius penalty on the prediction head to control \(L_h\). The encoder is an MoE message passer plus vector quantization; those parts are not described as spectrally normalized. — [arXiv:2602.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09258)
- Spectral norms in bounds, not as a regularizer. Sun and Lin (2024) and Liang et al. (2026) put \(\|W_i\|_2\), Frobenius terms, and \(\|P_G\|_2\) into generalization bounds. The pages that were read do not describe spectral normalization, orthogonal initialization, or a penalty used at training time. — [arXiv:2402.04038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04038); [arXiv:2606.06293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06293)
- Adjacent Lipschitz penalty outside classification certificates. Stability of Flow Models for Graph Signals (Martin Schmidt, Gonzalo Mateos; arXiv 8 Jul 2026; submitted to IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing) penalizes the spatial Lipschitz constant of a GNN-parameterized flow vector field so that relative graph perturbations move the generated distribution less. That is a generative stability penalty, not a node-classifier certificate. — [arXiv:2607.07510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07510)

### Inferences
- When these papers want a guarantee, they more often change the input distribution (smoothing or subgraph votes) or the aggregator (Chebyshev, sort, sliced Wasserstein, doubly stochastic mix) than they penalize \(\|W\|_2\) during SGD.
- Orthogonal weights were discussed as the wrong invariant for deep GNN mixing (mHC-GNN) and did not appear as a robustness regularizer in the certificate papers.

### Gaps
- A training recipe that spectrally normalizes every GraphSAGE layer and then certifies the resulting message-passing Lipschitz constant was not found. The zero-hit arXiv query for “spectral normalization” plus “graph neural” is consistent with that, and it is not a proof of absence after the rate limit.
- Soft orthogonal GNNs appear in NCGC (Songbo Wang, Renchi Yang, Yurui Lai, Xiaoyang Lin, Tsz Nam Chan; arXiv 25 Nov 2025) as a clustering architecture. The abstract does not state an adversarial certificate, and the paper was not read past the abstract, so it is not counted as a robustness method. — [arXiv:2511.19976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19976)
- MeixnerNet and DualLaguerreNet (Huseyin Goksu; arXiv 30 Oct 2025 and 4 Nov 2025) use discrete orthogonal polynomial spectral filters. The abstracts discuss heterophily and over-smoothing, not certified radii. They were not read past the abstracts. — [arXiv:2511.00113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00113); [arXiv:2511.13729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13729)

## What do authors say these methods fail to cover?

### Takeaway
Authors repeatedly say single-node smoothing certificates are pessimistic, that guarantees restricted to one edit type do not match attackers who change edges, nodes, and features together, and that a Lipschitz or spectral constraint that buys stability also suppresses the high-frequency signal needed for heterophily or worst-case generalization. Deterministic vote certificates can become vacuous when one injected node touches many edges, unless the partition is node-centric. PAC-Bayesian spectral bounds in this sample do not cover node classification or, in Sun and Lin, edge attacks.

### Cited Findings
- AuditVotes: vanilla randomized smoothing, including SparseSmooth, has a severe accuracy–robustness tradeoff; raising the edge-noise rate to enlarge the radius destroys clean accuracy; smoothed votes are inconsistent, so certified accuracy collapses even when some clean signal remains. Their filter proof is the part they say was missing, because an arbitrary filter can change the smoothing distribution. — [arXiv:2503.22998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22998)
- Collective injection certificate: sample-wise certificates assume the attacker may pick a different graph for each node, and they assume a fixed receptive field, which injection violates by adding edges. The LP is a relaxation; they claim it is a sound lower bound on the certified ratio, not the exact integer optimum. — [arXiv:2403.01423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01423)
- AGNNCert: edge-centric certificates are dominated by the number of edges incident to manipulated nodes, so an injected node with many neighbors can exceed the certified size even if few nodes were injected. A naive node partition gives node classification only one vote and “vacuous robustness.” They also say probabilistic certificates are not 100% accurate and that single-type threat models are impractical. The node-centric directed partition is their stated fix for the high-degree injection case. — [arXiv:2502.00765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00765)
- PGNNCert: the same three gaps (one perturbation type, one architecture or task, probabilistic guarantees), now for poisoning. The page says most certified GNN defenses target evasion, not training-time poisoning. — [arXiv:2503.18503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18503)
- Message-interception smoothing: black-box smoothing ignores message passing and is “overly pessimistic”; white-box certificates apply only to specific models and must be rederived for each new GNN. Their gray-box certificates still need Monte Carlo estimation of the smoothed classifier (they say only a few samples). They also say graph sparsification changes how large a certificate they can issue, which means the guarantee depends on a modified graph, not only on the original model. — [arXiv:2301.02039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02039)
- SpecSphere: single-branch certificates and static spectral–spatial fusion do not specialize structure robustness versus feature robustness; adversarial training against one threat model remains vulnerable to adaptive attacks. Their own consistency penalty fails in both directions: too small and the branches do not align, too large and the gate stops adapting. The certificate assumes spectral-norm bounds, a bound on \(\|\partial\hat{A}/\partial A\|_2\), and the threat set \(\|\Delta A\|_2\le\sqrt{2p}\) together with \(\ell_\infty\) features; edits outside that set are not claimed. — [arXiv:2505.08320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08320)
- FSW-GNN and the Hölder paper: standard sum MPNNs are not lower-Lipschitz, so WL separation can be smaller than floating-point resolution; lower Hölder exponents get worse with depth; SortMPNN’s padding requires a known maximum degree and excludes non-integer edge weights; bi-Lipschitz claims are for bounded-size graphs with bounded continuous features, not for adversarial edge-budget certificates. — [arXiv:2410.09118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09118); [arXiv:2406.06984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06984)
- Sun and Lin: the robust PAC-Bayes bound does not significantly cover edge attacks; it does not extend to node classification; it does not model the optimizer. They also say twice-differentiable Hessian analyses (Ju et al., 2023) exclude ReLU, which is a gap they claim to avoid, not a gap of their own bound. — [arXiv:2402.04038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04038)
- Liang et al.: prior robust PAC-Bayes analyses use isotropic Gaussians, perturb the full parameter space, scale with hidden width, treat GCN and message-passing models separately, and use coarse spectral factors for non-homogeneous message-passing weights. They do not claim a finite-sample certified radius for a deployed classifier. — [arXiv:2606.06293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06293)
- STEM-GNN: under a Lipschitz cap on the head, stability forces a ceiling on reliance on high-frequency features, and that ceiling leaves a nonzero worst-environment risk (Theorem 3.1). Instance-conditional routing without a stability control is unstable because perturbations can change the route. A single fixed low-pass rule cannot serve both homophilous and heterophilous regimes. — [arXiv:2602.09258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09258)
- mHC-GNN: removing the Sinkhorn constraint collapses training (the zero-variance numbers above). The orthogonal-subspace regime holds only while depth is below the number of streams. The paper does not claim robustness to adversarial edits. — [arXiv:2601.02451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02451)

### Inferences
- The failure mode shared by the smoothing papers and the Lipschitz papers is conservatism: either the certified radius shrinks until clean accuracy is gone, or the Lipschitz cap removes the signal the task needed. The 2024–2026 papers treat that as a structural tradeoff, not as a tuning error.
- Counting edges and counting nodes are not interchangeable. Authors of AGNNCert say an edge-centric certificate fails on high-degree injections even when the node budget looks small.

### Gaps
- Turning Strengths into Weaknesses (Binghui Wang, Meng Pang, Yun Dong; CVPR 2023; arXiv 10 Mar 2023) uses randomized-smoothing certified perturbation sizes as an attack score against evasion and poisoning. It is an attack paper, not a defense, and was not developed as a Lipschitz method. Abstract only. — [arXiv:2303.06199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06199)
- Graph Adversarial Immunization for Certifiable Robustness (Shuchang Tao, Huawei Shen, Qi Cao, Yunfan Wu, Liang Hou, Xueqi Cheng; arXiv 16 Feb 2023; comment: published in TKDE) improves a certificate by vaccinating edges or nodes. That is a graph edit, outside the Lipschitz and spectral-control slice, and the certificate construction itself was not re-read. The abstract’s reported robust-node ratio gains after immunizing 5% of nodes (79%, 294%, and 100% on three settings) are abstract numbers, not Lipschitz constants. — [arXiv:2302.08051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08051)
- RES-GRACE is cited by SpecSphere’s comparison table as a single-branch certificate for \((\ell_0,\ell_\infty)\). The paper was not opened, so its mechanism and limitations are unverified.
