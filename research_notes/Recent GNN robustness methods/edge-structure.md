# Structural and edge robustness of GNNs (2023–2026)

Scope: node-classification defenses against noisy, missing, or adversarially changed edges, plus robust message passing. Certified Lipschitz bounds, LLM semantic gates, and feature-shift / test-time adaptation are out of scope. Dates below are taken from arXiv PDFs, Crossref, OpenAlex, or DOI records retrieved on 4 October 2026. A number is included only when it was read from a PDF page or an abstract. If a venue banner was missing from the PDF, that is stated.

Pre-2023 methods that later papers still treat as the reference set (not re-reviewed here as primary entries): GNNGuard (Zhang and Zitnik, 2020; cosine gate described in Mujkanovic et al.), Jaccard-GCN (Wu et al., 2019, as cited by Mujkanovic et al.), RGCN, ProGNN (Jin et al., 2020, as cited by Hou et al.), SoftMedian (Geisler et al., NeurIPS 2021, “Robustness of Graph Neural Networks at Scale”), ElasticGNN (Liu et al., ICML 2021, as cited by Mujkanovic et al.), TWIRLS (Yang et al., cited by Hou et al.), EvenNet (used as a 2024–2026 baseline). The 2022 evaluation paper that later work still cites is Mujkanovic, Geisler, Günnemann, and Bojchevski, “Are Defenses for Graph Neural Networks Robust?” arXiv:2301.13694 (January 2023 preprint). Hou et al. and Lee and Park both cite it as 2022; Lee and Park’s bibliography lists it as Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 2022. OpenAlex records the arXiv copy as 2023. The NeurIPS 2022 proceedings page was not opened in this pass.

## Which 2024–2026 papers defend node classification against edge injection or edge perturbation, and how does the defense decide which edges to trust?

### Takeaway
The clearest 2024–2026 trust rules are: (1) RUNG zeros an edge when the normalized feature gap exceeds an MCP threshold and otherwise reweights it; (2) GPR-GAE replaces the graph with a self-supervised autoencoder reconstruction before the classifier runs; (3) TAGR adds a sparse feature-Gaussian neighborhood and reweights observed edges with a fixed mix of cosine, Jaccard, and local structure; (4) GJDNet down-weights neighbors that are skewness outliers in feature similarity. Several 2024–2025 papers still use Jaccard, link-prediction, or homophily filters. Most headline numbers are on Cora, Cora-ML, or Citeseer under topology attacks. Adaptive-attack numbers and random-noise numbers are not interchangeable.

### Cited Findings

**RUNG — robust aggregation / IRLS / edge reweighting.**
- Full title: “Robust Graph Neural Networks via Unbiased Aggregation.” Authors: Zhichao Hou, Ruiqi Feng, Tyler Derr, Xiaorui Liu. arXiv:2311.14934. Crossref indexes a proceedings version as Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37 (2024), DOI 10.52202/079017-3494. The arXiv PDF first page has no venue banner. Code: https://github.com/chris-hzc/RUNG. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14934); [Crossref DOI](https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-3494)
- Mechanism: they treat message passing as robust mean estimation, replace an ℓ1 penalty (which they show is biased as outliers accumulate) with a minimax concave penalty (MCP), and unroll a quasi-Newton iteratively reweighted least squares (QN-IRLS) solver as aggregation layers on an APPNP-style stack (MLP plus 10 aggregation layers). Edge weight \(W_{ij}\) is the derivative of the MCP penalty and becomes zero when the normalized feature difference \(y_{ij} \ge \gamma\), which prunes that edge. \(\hat\lambda = 1/(1+\lambda)\) is tuned in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9} and \(\gamma\) in {0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5}. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Threat: adaptive topology attacks (PGD evasion and poisoning, local and global), transfer attacks, and a graph injection setting following TDGIA. Local budget is edges perturbed relative to the target node’s degree. Global budget is a fraction of edges. Datasets in the main tables: Cora ML and Citeseer. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Headline, adaptive local evasion, Cora ML, node classification accuracy (mean ± std). Clean / 100% / 200% of target degree: RUNG 84.0±5.3 / 70.7±10.6 / 69.3±9.0. Same columns: GCN 82.7±4.9 / 2.7±2.5 / 0.0±0.0; GNNGuard 82.7±6.7 / 14.0±6.8 / 2.0±2.7; RGCN 84.6±4.0 / 6.0±3.9 / 0.0; ProGNN 84.7±6.2 / 4.0±2.5 / 0.0; Jaccard-GCN 81.3±5.0 / 4.7±3.4 / 0.7±1.3; SoftMedian 80.0±10.2 / 46.7±11.0 / 8.7±3.4; TWIRLS 83.3±7.3 / 36.0±8.8 / 12.0±6.9; TWIRLS-T 82.0±4.5 / 54.7±6.2 / 40.7±11.8; EvenNet 83.4±8.1 / 29.5±5.3 / 1.1±1.3; GARNET 82.4±6.8 / 42.7±9.3 / 9.6±3.5. — [arXiv PDF, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Headline, adaptive global attack, Cora ML, accuracy at 0% / 20% / 40% edge budget: RUNG 84.6±0.5 / 71.8±0.4 / 65.1±1.2. GCN 85.0±0.4 / 60.9±0.7 / 48.4±0.5. GNNGuard 83.1±0.7 / 63.1±1.1 / 51.0±1.2. ProGNN 85.6±0.5 / 63.0±0.7 / 51.3±0.6. Jaccard-GCN 83.7±0.7 / 60.0±1.1 / 49.1±2.4. RGCN 85.7±0.4 / 59.8±0.7 / 46.1±0.7. SoftMedian 85.0±0.7 / 69.5±0.5 / 58.1±0.7. — [arXiv PDF, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)

**GPR-GAE — purification before the classifier.**
- Full title: “Self-supervised Adversarial Purification for Graph Neural Networks.” Authors: Woohyun Lee, Hogun Park. arXiv:2605.23239 (2026). No venue is printed on the PDF. Code: https://github.com/woodavid31/GPR-GAE. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23239)
- Mechanism: a graph autoencoder (GPR-GAE) is trained self-supervised with several Generalized PageRank filters and then purifies the adjacency in multiple steps. The classifier trains and predicts on the cleaned graph. Trust is the reconstructed edge set, not a hand-set Jaccard cutoff. Hop mix is a separate learned GPR coefficient. Training corruption samples edge drops, additions, and reweighting from a budget \((p,q,\eta)\); the reported setting is \((1.5, 0.2, 3)\) and \(K=7\). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- Threat: adaptive structural attacks PRBCD and LRBCD (global budget \(\epsilon\) times degree mass of targeted nodes), plus transfer PRBCD from a vanilla GCN. Node classification. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- Headline (prose, Cora): adversarially trained GPRGNN under PRBCD with training \(\epsilon=0.2\) reaches 48.5% test accuracy against PRBCD at \(\epsilon=0.5\); GPR-GAE attached to vanilla GPRGNN is 15.7 points higher. Largest clean-to-attack drop they state for LRBCD is 8.9 points (GPR-GAE with vanilla GAT). Largest clean-accuracy drop they state for adding the purifier is 0.9 points (self-trained GCN). — [arXiv PDF, p. 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- Extracted Table 1 cells, Cora test accuracy. SoftMedianGDC clean 77.4±1.8, PRBCD \(\epsilon=0.5\) 48.2±1.1. EvenNet clean 81.4±2.0, PRBCD \(\epsilon=0.5\) 35.6±0.9. GPR-GAE + vanilla GCN: clean 79.4±0.3; LRBCD/PRBCD at \(\epsilon=0.1\) are 75.9±2.0 and 75.6±2.1; at \(\epsilon=0.5\), 72.5±1.7 and 61.3±3.6. Column order follows the table header (LRBCD then PRBCD at 0.1, 0.25, 0.5). Underlines in the PDF were extracted as colon runs and ignored. — [arXiv PDF, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- Transfer (non-adaptive) Table 2, clean / \(\epsilon=0.25\) / \(\epsilon=0.5\): Citeseer GPR-GAE+vanilla GCN 71.2 / 68.7 / 66.4 versus vanilla GCN 71.5 / 40.0 / 25.3. Pubmed 75.1 / 73.5 / 72.0 versus 75.1 / 37.1 / 24.1. OGB-arXiv 69.1 / 49.2 / 42.3 versus 70.8 / 43.1 / 33.1. SVD-GCN is marked OOM on OGB-arXiv. — [arXiv PDF, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)

**TAGR — feature-neighborhood repair plus topology reweighting.**
- Full title: “Topology-Aware Gaussian Graph Repair for Robust Graph Neural Networks.” Authors: Anubha Goel, Juho Kanniainen (Tampere University). arXiv:2606.03462 (2 June 2026). No venue on the PDF. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03462)
- Mechanism: before training, build a sparse graph of feature-similar nodes with a per-node Gaussian bandwidth, and reweight each observed edge by a fixed score \(q_{ij} = 1.50\,z(\mathrm{cosine}) + 0.50\,z(\mathrm{Jaccard}) + 0.25\,z(\log(1+M_{ij})) + 0.10\,z(\kappa_i+\kappa_j) - 0.10\,z(|d_i-d_j|)\), where \(M_{ij}\) is a common-neighbor term and \(z\) is standardization. The repaired adjacency is frozen and passed to an unmodified GNN. — [arXiv PDF, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)
- Threat in the experiments that were extracted: random edge addition and random edge deletion on citation graphs, plus clean accuracy. Adaptive / poisoning attackers were not described in the extracted sections. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)
- Headline numbers that were extracted: Cora clean test accuracy, TAGR-GCN 80.5±0.7 versus GCN 79.9±0.7 (Table 2, row truncated after that). Cora-ML edge deletion: TAGR-GCN 81.3% at 25% deletion versus Gaussian-only GCN 80.6%, and 78.4% versus 78.1% at 50% deletion. Clean Cornell: TAGR-GCN 0.8203±0.0581 versus GCN 0.2270±0.0308. Clean Flickr: TAGR-GCN 0.8797±0.018 versus GCN 0.6382±0.0085 (Table 4). They also report TAGR-GCN has the best average rank across addition and deletion curves. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)

**GJDNet — soft neighbor filtering (and a separate decision geometry).**
- Full title: “GJDNet: Robust Graph Neural Networks via Joint Disentangled Learning Against Adversarial Attacks.” Authors: Canyixing Cui, Tao Wu, Xingping Xian, Xiao-Ke Xu, Mao Wang, Weina Niu. arXiv:2606.01560 (1 June 2026). The PDF uses a generic LaTeX journal template. No peer-reviewed venue was verified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01560)
- Mechanism: feature cosine drives a soft structural disentanglement; a skewness check on the neighborhood similarity distribution filters extreme neighbors (low-similarity outliers or abnormally high-similarity neighbors). A spherical decision boundary then tightens class regions in embedding space. Trust at the edge level is the skewness-aware filter, not a global Jaccard threshold. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)
- Threat: “min-max” structural attacks on assortative and disassortative graphs. This was not checked against the Mujkanovic adaptive-PGD protocol. — [arXiv PDF, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)
- Headline, Cora accuracy at perturbation rate 0 / 0.10 / 0.25: GJDNet 0.8564 / 0.8329 / 0.7770. GCN 0.8325 / 0.7995 / 0.7388. GCN-Jaccard 0.8370 / 0.8035 / 0.7422. RGCN 0.8422 / 0.8106 / 0.7450. Citeseer at rate 0.25: GJDNet 0.7007 versus GCN 0.6771. Standard deviations were not in the extracted table text. — [arXiv PDF, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)

### Inferences
- For an adaptive edge-perturbation attacker who can see the defense, the 2024 method with a published head-to-head against GNNGuard, Jaccard-GCN, RGCN, ProGNN, SoftMedian, and TWIRLS is RUNG, and its trust decision is a hard threshold on feature difference inside an IRLS reweighting, not a preprocessing Jaccard sweep.
- For a plug-in cleaner in front of an existing GNN, the 2026 method with adaptive PRBCD/LRBCD numbers is GPR-GAE. TAGR is the lightweight alternative when the corruption model is random add/delete rather than a gradient attack.
- Injection-specific defenses in this slice (Tong et al. 2024; RUNG’s TDGIA appendix) are fewer than modification defenses, and only RUNG’s Citeseer injection table had extracted accuracies.

### Gaps
- Full main-table numbers were not extracted for ADEdgeDrop, GRANCE, ACGMAE, Pure-GNN, BDP, Tong et al., GRN, PRDM, Li and Liu, Mid-GCN, MHR-GCN, or IDEA.
- GJDNet’s “min-max” attack was not confirmed to be an adaptive attack in the Mujkanovic sense.
- TAGR’s experiments that were read are random edge add/delete, so they do not answer adaptive edge injection.
- “Evolutionary graph structure learning for adversarial defense in GNNs” (Hong Pan, Jingwei Guo, Liang Cheng, Rufei Gao, Xiaodan Sui, International Journal of Machine Learning and Cybernetics, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s13042-026-03230-1) had no abstract in OpenAlex, so its trust rule is unknown.
- The original NoisyGNN, GARNET, and HANG papers were not opened. They appear only as RUNG/DSGNN baselines.

## What succeeded GNNGuard, Jaccard, RGCN, and ProGNN?

### Takeaway
Those four are still the baseline row, and under adaptive attacks they are close to an undefended GCN. The methods that later papers actually put above them are RUNG (QN-IRLS / MCP, 2024), SoftMedian and TWIRLS (2021, still the strong ℓ1 baselines inside the RUNG paper), EvenNet (spectral, still a 2026 baseline), GPR-GAE (2026 purifier), GJDNet (2026, on a min-max attack), and DSGNN (2025, on that paper’s average). ElasticGNN is grouped by RUNG with SoftMedian and TWIRLS as the ℓ1 family whose bias RUNG is designed to remove. It is not a 2024–2026 successor.

### Cited Findings
- Mujkanovic et al. evaluate seven defenses: Jaccard-GCN, SVD-GCN, ProGNN, GRAND, GNNGuard, RGCN, and Soft-Median-GDC. Taxonomy in their Table 1: unsupervised graph improvement = Jaccard-GCN and SVD-GCN; supervised graph improvement = ProGNN; rule-based adaptive edge weights = GNNGuard; robust aggregation includes Soft-Median. On Cora ML, about half of the defenses are worse than undefended GCN under the strongest global attack at a given budget (their Figure 4). Adaptive SVD-GCN on one global setting reaches (R)AUC below 0.02. They report that non-adaptive attacks make the same defenses look robust. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13694)
- Their appendix states GNNGuard’s rule as four steps starting from layer-wise cosine similarity of embeddings, keeping edges with cosine at least 0.1, then rescaling by a degree-like normalizer. Soft-Median-GDC replaces the weighted mean with weights from a softmax of distance to a dimension-wise weighted median of the neighborhood. — [arXiv PDF, appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13694)
- Hou et al. write that SoftMedian, TWIRLS, and ElasticGNN look different but are ℓ1 robust estimators. TWIRLS is the ElasticGNN objective with \(\lambda_2=0\) and \(p=2\), up to degree normalization, solved by IRLS; ElasticGNN uses PAPC. Under adaptive local attack their curves align, and all three sit far below RUNG once the budget is large (Table 1: at 200% of degree on Cora ML, SoftMedian 8.7±3.4 and TWIRLS 12.0±6.9 versus RUNG 69.3±9.0; GNNGuard 2.0±2.7, ProGNN 0.0, Jaccard-GCN 0.7±1.3, RGCN 0.0). On the global 40% budget the gap is smaller but the same order: RUNG 65.1±1.2, SoftMedian 58.1±0.7, TWIRLS 58.7±1.1, GNNGuard 51.0±1.2, ProGNN 51.3±0.6, Jaccard-GCN 49.1±2.4, RGCN 46.1±0.7, GCN 48.4±0.5. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Lee and Park (2026) still list EvenNet, SoftMedianGDC, and Jaccard-GCN as the robust baselines. On their Cora adaptive table, SoftMedianGDC at PRBCD \(\epsilon=0.5\) is 48.2±1.1 and EvenNet is 35.6±0.9, while GPR-GAE+vanilla GCN is 61.3±3.6. On transfer attacks, Jaccard-GCN on Citeseer at \(\epsilon=0.5\) is 40.6 versus GPR-GAE+GCN 66.4. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- GJDNet’s Cora min-max table at rate 0.25: GJDNet 0.7770, GCN-Jaccard 0.7422, RGCN 0.7450, GCN 0.7388. The margin over Jaccard and RGCN is about three to four points, much smaller than RUNG’s margin under adaptive local PGD. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)
- DSGNN’s abstract says its average accuracy exceeds GNNGuard, GCN-Jaccard, GCN-SVD, RGCN, and NoisyGNN, with averages 81.24% (Cora), 71.94% (CiteSeer), 81.66% (Industry). — [CMC abstract via OpenAlex](https://doi.org/10.32604/cmc.2025.067284)
- MHR-GCN (2023) is an explicit patch on homophily truncation (the Jaccard / GNNGuard family): keep a truncated 2-hop neighborhood because 1-hop truncation drops useful edges. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn54540.2023.10191493)
- Mid-GCN (WWW 2023) is the spectral successor in this window: a mid-pass filter instead of a learned clean adjacency. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583335)

### Inferences
- “What succeeded GNNGuard / Jaccard / RGCN / ProGNN” in the adaptive-attack literature is RUNG’s unbiased MCP aggregation, not another preprocessing prune. SoftMedian and TWIRLS already beat those four on Hou et al.’s global attack, and RUNG is the 2024 method that then beats SoftMedian and TWIRLS when the local budget is large.
- GPR-GAE is the 2026 purifier that reports gains over SoftMedianGDC, EvenNet, and Jaccard-GCN under PRBCD/LRBCD. It does not report GNNGuard, RGCN, or ProGNN in the extracted tables.
- GJDNet and DSGNN name Jaccard and RGCN as baselines and report smaller or aggregated gains. They should not be described as having matched RUNG’s adaptive-local margin unless a shared attack script is run.

### Gaps
- ElasticGNN does not have a numeric row in the extracted RUNG Tables 1–2. It is only grouped with SoftMedian and TWIRLS in the Figure 1 discussion.
- No 2024–2026 paper in this pass reports a single shared leaderboard of RUNG versus GPR-GAE versus GJDNet versus DSGNN.
- ProGNN’s original low-rank plus feature-smoothness objective was not re-read; only its role as a weak adaptive-attack baseline was verified.

## Do any papers separate “how much neighborhood influence” from “which neighbor gets it,” or combine structural signals with an external score?

### Takeaway
Yes. RUNG splits a hard keep/drop (MCP weight hits zero past \(\gamma\)) from a scalar smoothing weight \(\lambda\). TAGR splits “which new neighbor” (Gaussian feature kNN) from “how much the observed edge counts” (a weighted sum of cosine, Jaccard, common neighbors, and degree gap). GPR-GAE splits hop mix (GPR coefficients) from the purified edge set. GJDNet’s spherical boundary is a decision-space weight, separate from the neighbor filter. The external scores that showed up are feature cosine or Gaussian feature distance, link prediction (Tong et al.), an SVD low-rank graph (Li and Liu), and a line-graph adversarial predictor (ADEdgeDrop). No paper in this slice uses an LLM or a certified Lipschitz score as that external signal.

### Cited Findings
- RUNG, interpretability paragraph: \(W_{ij}^{(k)}\) reweights the normalized adjacency and is zero once the node difference exceeds \(\gamma\), “thus pruning suspicious edges.” The skip term \(F^{(0)}\) and \(\mathrm{diag}(q^{(k)})+\lambda I\) set how strongly the estimate stays at the original features. \(\gamma\) and \(\lambda\) are tuned separately. Appendix text says a small \(\gamma\) hurts clean accuracy more than it helps robustness, and \(\gamma \to \infty\) recovers an ℓ1 model that degrades at 20–40% global budgets. — [arXiv PDF, pp. 6–7 and appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Hou et al. also write a GCN variant \(F^{(k+1)}=\mathrm{ReLU}((W^{(k)}\odot \tilde A) F^{(k)} M^{(k)})\), so the robust weights \(W\) (which neighbor) multiply a learned feature map \(M\) (how the message is transformed). On Cora ML global budgets, RUNG-GCN is weaker than decoupled RUNG; the extracted appendix row starts RUNG-ℓ1-GCN at clean 84.0±0.4 and 5% 74.7±0.6, versus decoupled RUNG 84.6±0.5 and 78.9±0.4. The full GCN-variant row was truncated. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- TAGR: new edges come only from the Gaussian feature neighborhood and are excluded if they already exist; observed edges are only rescaled by \(R_\lambda\). Feature agreement has the largest coefficient (1.50) and Jaccard the next (0.50). Setting the residual multiplier \(\lambda=0\) recovers Gaussian-only repair, which they say is the main gain; the structural residual is a stabilizer. Cora-ML deletion gains over Gaussian-only are 0.7 points at 25% deletion and 0.3 points at 50%. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)
- GPR-GAE: each GPR filter has per-hop coefficients (how much each neighborhood radius contributes). Purification then decides the edge set. They plot learned coefficients on Cora and Chameleon (Figure 4); the numeric coefficient table was not extracted. They state the filters are meant to cover homophilic and heterophilic graphs. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- GJDNet: neighbor filtering uses the skewness of feature-similarity inside the neighborhood (which neighbors survive). \(L_{\mathrm{SDB}}=L_{\mathrm{intra}}/(L_{\mathrm{inter}}+\delta)\) then changes the decision regions without being an edge weight. Ablation text: accuracy falls when the number of subspaces \(K\) or iterations \(T\) is too large; they report a good range \(K \in [3,5]\), \(T \in [6,10]\). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)
- ACGMAE: a probability that a neighbor is a true positive is computed on top of a kNN graph versus the attacked graph, which is an external feature-space score multiplied into the contrastive pair. — [AAAI abstract](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i12.33364)
- Pure-GNN keeps every edge and only changes importance weights, plus a residual on the representation. That is “how much,” with “which” fixed to the observed graph. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26599/tst.2025.9010034)
- ADEdgeDrop’s line-graph predictor outputs which edges to drop. Retained edges are not described as continuously reweighted in the abstract. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09171)
- Tong et al.: a link-prediction model is the external score for injected edges, combined with a homogeneity filter. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/dsc63484.2024.00031)
- Li and Liu: node-feature similarity and an SVD low-rank graph are fused, then an RL policy chooses the defense action. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3729706.3729715)
- BDP and MHR-GCN use Jaccard or homophily as a hard keep/drop, which does not separate a continuous influence weight from membership, except that MHR-GCN also chooses a second hop radius. — [BDP](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn60899.2024.10650844); [MHR-GCN](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn54540.2023.10191493)
- DSGNN’s two channels separate structure noise from feature noise, then an adaptive fusion sets how much each channel contributes. That is a channel weight, not a per-neighbor trust score. The abstract does not give the fusion formula. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.32604/cmc.2025.067284)

### Inferences
- The papers that literally factor the two questions are RUNG (\(\gamma\) versus \(\lambda\)) and TAGR (Gaussian candidate set versus residual edge score). GPR-GAE factors hop radius versus edge existence, which is a coarser version of the same split.
- The structural signals combined with a non-structural score are Jaccard or common neighbors plus feature cosine (TAGR, BDP, MHR-GCN, GNNGuard’s cosine as described by Mujkanovic et al.), link prediction (Tong et al.), and SVD (Li and Liu; also the older SVD-GCN baseline).

### Gaps
- Exact equations for DSGNN’s fusion, Tong et al.’s link-prediction threshold, and Pure-GNN’s importance function were not retrieved.
- No paper here was found that multiplies a structural weight by a score from a model trained on a different domain (text encoder, retrieval score). That combination was left to the other slices on purpose.

## What failure modes do the papers themselves admit (adaptive attacks, heterophily, scalability)?

### Takeaway
The failure mode the 2024–2026 papers repeat is evaluation under non-adaptive or transfer attacks. RUNG’s own tables show GNNGuard, Jaccard-GCN, RGCN, and ProGNN collapsing under adaptive local PGD, and they show ℓ1 methods (SoftMedian, TWIRLS, RUNG-ℓ1) biasing as the attack budget grows. Heterophily is an explicit design target for GJDNet and a claimed operating regime for GPR filters and TAGR’s Cornell result, but RUNG’s extracted experiments are Cora ML and Citeseer. Scalability limits that are written down: GPR-GAE adaptive gradients do not run on OGB-arXiv, SVD-GCN OOMs there, and ADEdgeDrop’s table marks 1-day timeouts and 16G out-of-memory.

### Cited Findings
- Adaptive attacks. Mujkanovic et al.: of 49 robust-GNN papers at major venues, essentially none were evaluated with adaptive attacks; the seven representative defenses are only marginally better than GCN, and some are worse, once the attack is adaptive. They release perturbed graphs as a black-box unit test. They also drop ProGNN’s nuclear-norm term when attacking it because it is expensive and non-differentiable. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13694)
- Hou et al. cite that result and say transfer attacks are weaker than attacks on the victim, so a transfer-only evaluation gives a false sense of security. Their Figure 7 claim is that RUNG pushes the adaptive attacker onto edges with small feature difference; edges that connect dissimilar nodes get pruned. They also show ℓ1 estimators accumulate bias as the outlier ratio grows (Figure 2 simulation and Figure 6 on attacked graphs), which is the failure mode of SoftMedian, TWIRLS, ElasticGNN, and their own RUNG-ℓ1 ablation. At 200% local budget RUNG itself falls from 84.0±5.3 clean to 69.3±9.0, with a large standard deviation. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)
- Lee and Park: adversarial training inside one classifier trades accuracy for robustness (they measure a 1.6 point clean drop for PRBCD-trained GPRGNN versus self-trained GPRGNN). Non-differentiable purifiers (Jaccard-GCN, SVD-GCN, GOOD-AT) cannot be attacked by the same gradient attack, so those comparisons are transfer-only. On OGB-arXiv they write that GPR-GAE can purify in batches but gradient-based adaptive attacks cannot, so adaptive attacks there are “unscalable”; they report transfer only. SVD-GCN and GOOD-AT are OOM on OGB-arXiv in Table 2. Multi-step purification cost is stated as node encoding \(O(K \cdot |E| \cdot Z)\) per step. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- Heterophily. GJDNet’s abstract: attacks invert assortativity (disassortative edges on assortative graphs and the reverse), and defenses that assume one assortativity regime mishandle the other. Their Table II includes both regimes; only Cora, Citeseer, and the start of Pubmed were extracted. They also report rejection-perturbation coverage under a 5% min-max attack, including heterophilous graphs: Cora 68.40%, Citeseer 57.92%, Pubmed 66.54%, Cornell 77.78%, Texas 57.14%, Wisconsin 69.23%, Amazon 74.32%, Actor 45.91% (Figure 8). What “RPC” counts was not re-derived. Too-large \(K\) or \(T\) “accumulate noise.” — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01560)
- TAGR: under random edge addition, both homophily of the repaired graph and accuracy fall together (Figure 5). They write that TAGR-GCN “is not always the best method in every single high-noise setting.” Gaussian feature repair, not the structural residual, is the main gain. The repaired graph is fixed before training, so it cannot adapt to a training-time attacker who sees the repair. Cornell (a standard heterophilous graph) is only reported clean: TAGR-GCN 0.8203±0.0581 versus GCN 0.2270±0.0308. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03462)
- GPR-GAE includes Chameleon as the heterophilic dataset in Figure 3 and states that learned GPR coefficients adapt across homophily and heterophily. Numeric Chameleon accuracies were not extracted from the figure. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23239)
- MHR-GCN: homophily truncation “suffers the problem of losing much effective neighborhood information,” which caps both clean and attacked accuracy. Their theory claim is about graph structure attacks on homophilic truncation, not about heterophilic graphs. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn54540.2023.10191493)
- Mid-GCN: purification and robustness-constraint defenses are “usually designed for specific data or attacks,” add an objective that is not the classification loss, and cost extra training. That is their stated reason to use a filter instead. They do not, in the abstract, report an adaptive-attack failure of Mid-GCN itself. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3543507.3583335)
- GRANCE: global-local graph contrastive learning “fails to address the robustness issues inherent in the contexts of adversarial robustness,” which is why they switch to local neighborhood contrast. — [AAAI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i12.33471)
- ADEdgeDrop: random edge dropping removes critical edges. Their own table records OOT at 1 day and OOM at 16G, so the line-graph predictor does not finish on at least some of BlogCatalog, Pubmed, ACM, Chameleon, UAI, Actor, CoraFull, and Arxiv. Which cells are OOT/OOM was not extracted. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09171)
- BDP: existing defenses “cannot recover the implicit relationships” of a bipartite graph after an attack; deletion-only purification is the failure they target. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ijcnn60899.2024.10650844)
- Pure-GNN: deleting adversarial edges fails to preserve graph integrity and does not by itself improve representation power. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26599/tst.2025.9010034)
- DSGNN: joint structure-and-feature perturbations are “significantly more challenging than isolated attacks.” They claim a tighter robustness bound than single-channel GNNs. An author-stated failure of DSGNN (heterophily, adaptive attack, scale) was not in the abstract. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.32604/cmc.2025.067284)
- Hou et al. complexity paragraph: QN-IRLS uses a diagonal Hessian approximation; total cost \(O((m+n)kd)\). They present this as remaining efficient, not as a scaling failure. Experiments extracted are Cora ML and Citeseer, not OGB-arXiv. — [arXiv PDF, appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.14934)

### Inferences
- Any defense whose only published numbers are transfer or random edge flips (TAGR’s extracted protocol, several purification abstracts) is exactly the evaluation pattern Mujkanovic et al. and Hou et al. say inflates robustness.
- Heterophily is handled by changing the assumption (GJDNet; GPR hop mix; TAGR’s feature edges) more often than by a published adaptive-attack table on Cornell, Texas, or Actor. The Actor RPC of 45.91% is the weakest coverage number GJDNet prints in that figure, but it is not an accuracy.
- Scale is admitted where the attack or the line graph is dense (OGB-arXiv adaptive gradients; ADEdgeDrop OOM/OOT; SVD-GCN OOM), not where the defense is a sparse frozen repair (TAGR).

### Gaps
- RUNG limitations on heterophily and on graphs larger than Citeseer were not found in the extracted pages. Absence from those pages is not a proof that the paper never discusses them.
- Chameleon numbers for GPR-GAE and the per-attack DSGNN tables were not read.
- Whether GJDNet, DSGNN, Pure-GNN, GRANCE, or BDP remain strong under the Mujkanovic adaptive attack is unanswered here.
- RS-Pool (Ennadir et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2510.22643) is about graph-classification pooling under adversarial attacks, so it was left out of this edge-trust slice. The abstract’s numeric claim was not extracted.
