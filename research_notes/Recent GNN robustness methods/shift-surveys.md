# Shift, adaptation, and robustness surveys

Scope note. Papers below are from OpenAlex title-filtered queries and ar5iv HTML fetched on 4 October 2026. Citation counts are OpenAlex `cited_by_count` from those queries, not a field-wide ranking. An unfiltered citation sort returned unrelated highly cited papers and was discarded. Abstracts quoted or paraphrased below come from OpenAlex reconstructed abstracts or from ar5iv text. ## What mechanisms do 2024–2026 papers use for feature missingness, covariate shift, degree shift, or homophily shift?

### Takeaway

2024–2026 work does not share one mechanism. Incomplete features are handled by teacher–student distillation or dual-stream reconstruction plus contrastive learning; distribution shift is handled by causal subgraph reweighting, frozen-model test-time feature reconstruction, or contrastive pseudo-observations; degree bias is handled by test-time virtual-node patching or self-distillation plus graph completion; homophily/heterophily is handled by edge splitting or two-fold spectral filtering. The phrase “homophily shift” did not appear as a paper title in these searches; the literature uses heterophily and degree bias instead.

### Cited Findings

**Feature missingness**

- T2-GNN trains separate feature-level and structure-level teacher models and distills both into a student GNN (for example a GCN) with a dual distillation mode, so feature completion and structure completion do not interfere when both are incomplete. Full title: “T2-GNN: Graph Neural Networks for Graphs with Incomplete Features and Structure via Teacher-Student Distillation.” Authors (OpenAlex): Cuiying Huo; Di Jin; Yawen Li; Dongxiao He; Yu-Bin Yang; Lingfei Wu. Year 2023. Venue: Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence. OpenAlex citations: 49. Problem: graphs with incomplete features and structure. — [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v37i4.25553](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v37i4.25553)
- MDS-GNN reconstructs missing node features from the incomplete structure, builds an augmented global graph from those features, propagates incomplete features on that graph, and uses contrastive learning so the two streams benefit each other. Full title: “MDS-GNN: A Mutual Dual-Stream Graph Neural Network on Graphs with Incomplete Features and Structure.” Authors (OpenAlex): Peng Yuan; Peng Tang. Year 2024. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 1. — [arXiv:2408.04845](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04845)

**Covariate / distribution shift (authors’ wording; not all papers say “covariate”)**

- StableGNN, in “Generalizing Graph Neural Networks on Out-of-Distribution Graphs,” pools the graph into subgraph representations and applies a causal variable-distinguishing regularizer that learns sample weights to correct a biased training distribution, so the model relies less on spurious subgraph–label correlations. Authors (OpenAlex): Shaohua Fan; Xiao Wang; Chuan Shi; Peng Cui; Bai Wang. Year 2023. Venue: IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence. OpenAlex citations: 93. Problem: agnostic distribution shift between train and test graphs. — [DOI 10.1109/tpami.2023.3321097](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2023.3321097)
- FR-GNN keeps a trained GNN frozen. It maps the model’s outputs back toward inputs to form class-representative embeddings, reconstructs features of labeled nodes, and feeds those reconstructed features into message passing so unlabeled test nodes are predicted under a reduced shift, with no change to architecture or parameters. Journal title: “FR-GNN: Mitigating the Impact of Distribution Shift on Graph Neural Networks via Test-Time Feature Reconstruction.” Authors (OpenAlex, journal record): Rui Ding; Jielong Yang; Feng Quan Ji; Xionghu Zhong; Linbo Xie. Year 2024. Venue: IEEE Internet of Things Journal. OpenAlex citations: 4. The 2023 arXiv record “FRGNN: …” lists Feng Ji rather than “Feng Quan Ji” and has 0 OpenAlex citations. — [DOI 10.1109/jiot.2024.3385292](https://doi.org/10.1109/jiot.2024.3385292); [arXiv:2308.09259](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09259)
- COIN-GNN selects stable entities and spatial relations with a Relation Importance metric, stores influential timestamps in a temporal memory, generates pseudo-observations for unstable entities, and trains with contrastive learning so inductive spatial-temporal prediction does not need future knowledge of new entities. Full title: “COIN-GNN: Inductive Spatial-Temporal Prediction for Continuous Distribution Shifts via Graph Neural Networks.” Authors (OpenAlex): Jialun Zheng; Divya Saxena; Jiannong Cao. Year 2025. Venue: IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering. OpenAlex citations: 2. — [DOI 10.1109/tkde.2025.3606629](https://doi.org/10.1109/tkde.2025.3606629)
- Bazhenov et al. do not propose a defense. They induce structural distributional shifts by splitting nodes on popularity, locality, and density, and report that these splits are hard and that simpler models often beat more elaborate ones. Full title: “Evaluating Robustness and Uncertainty of Graph Models Under Structural Distributional Shifts.” Authors (OpenAlex): Gleb Bazhenov; Denis Kuznedelev; Andrey Malinin; Artem Babenko; Liudmila Prokhorenkova. Year 2023. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 1. — [arXiv:2302.13875](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13875)
- GNNSafe is an OOD detector, not a training defense: an energy score read from a GNN trained with standard classification loss, optionally sharpened by learning-free energy belief propagation. Full title: “Energy-based Out-of-Distribution Detection for Graph Neural Networks.” Authors (OpenAlex): Qitian Wu; Yi-Ting Chen; Chenxiao Yang; Junchi Yan. Year 2023. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 8. The abstract reports up to 17.0% AUROC improvement over the baselines they compare, on synthetic shifts plus cross-domain and temporal graph shifts. — [arXiv:2302.02914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.02914)

**Degree bias (the retrieved papers say “degree bias,” not “degree shift”)**

- GraphPatcher is model-agnostic test-time augmentation: it iteratively adds virtual nodes that patch artificially corrupted low-degree nodes and is trained to reconstruct the target GNN’s predictions along that corruption sequence, so low-degree nodes improve without a training regime that only sees low-degree nodes. Full title: “GraphPatcher: Mitigating Degree Bias for Graph Neural Networks via Test-time Augmentation.” Authors (OpenAlex): Mingxuan Ju; Tong Zhao; Wenhao Yu; Neil Shah; Yanfang Ye. Year 2023. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 4. The abstract states gains of up to 3.6% overall and up to 6.5% on low-degree nodes across seven datasets. — [arXiv:2310.00800](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.00800)
- Grace attributes low-degree failure to weak self-representations and low neighborhood homophily, then uses self-distillation, graph completion to raise that homophily ratio, and label propagation to limit completion errors. Full title: “Grace: Graph Self-Distillation and Completion to Mitigate Degree-Related Biases.” Authors (OpenAlex): Hui Xu; Liyao Xiang; Femke Huang; Yuting Weng; Ruijie Xu; Xinbing Wang; Chenghu Zhou. Year 2023. OpenAlex venue field was empty; DOI is an ACM record. OpenAlex citations: 8. — [DOI 10.1145/3580305.3599368](https://doi.org/10.1145/3580305.3599368)
- Subramonian, Kang, and Sun analyze message-passing GNNs rather than proposing a new architecture. They say a survey of 38 degree-bias papers found hypotheses that are often unvalidated or contradictory; they prove high-degree test nodes tend to have a lower misclassification probability regardless of training, and they tie the bias to degree-associated factors such as neighbor homophily and neighbor diversity. Full title: “Theoretical and Empirical Insights into the Origins of Degree Bias in Graph Neural Networks.” Year 2024. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 1. — [arXiv:2404.03139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03139)

**Homophily / heterophily (no title hit for “homophily shift”)**

- ES-GNN alternately splits edges into two complementary subgraphs on the same nodes and propagates on each, separating task-relevant from task-irrelevant features. The authors describe this as a disentangled graph-denoising solution and also report robustness on adversarial graphs and less over-smoothing. Full title: “ES-GNN: Generalizing Graph Neural Networks Beyond Homophily With Edge Splitting.” Authors (OpenAlex): Jingwei Guo; Kaizhu Huang; Rui Zhang; Xinping Yi. Year 2024. Venue: IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence. OpenAlex citations: 22. — [DOI 10.1109/tpami.2024.3459932](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2024.3459932); [arXiv PDF linked by OpenAlex](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13700)
- PC-Conv / PCNet extends the graph heat equation for long-range heterophilic aggregation, approximates that filter with Poisson–Charlier polynomials, and uses the resulting convolution at multiple orders so one model covers both homophilic and heterophilic graphs. Full title: “PC-Conv: Unifying Homophily and Heterophily with Two-Fold Filtering.” Authors (OpenAlex): Bingheng Li; Erlin Pan; Zhao Kang. Year 2024. Venue: Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence. OpenAlex citations: 46. — [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i12.29246](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i12.29246)
- SEC-GFD is a fraud detector, not a general shift method. It uses hybrid spectral filtering that splits the spectrum into mixed-frequency bands using the link between spectral energy and heterophily, plus a local environmental constraint to use scarce labels. Full title: “Revisiting Graph-Based Fraud Detection in Sight of Heterophily and Spectrum.” Authors as listed by OpenAlex: Fan L. Xu; Nan Wang; Hao Wu; Xuezhi Wen; Xibin Zhao; Hai Wan. Year 2024. Venue: AAAI. OpenAlex citations: 57. — [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i8.28773](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i8.28773)

**Organizing taxonomy for heterophily (used by later method papers)**

- Zheng et al. group heterophilic GNNs into (1) non-local neighbor extension, (2) architecture refinement, and (3) hybrids. Non-local extension splits into high-order neighbor mixing and potential-neighbor discovery (structure distance, feature distance, or hybrid distance). Architecture refinement splits into identifiable message aggregation (edge weight, feature weight, or hybrid weight) and inter-layer combination. Full title: “Graph Neural Networks for Graphs With Heterophily: A Survey.” Authors on ar5iv: Xin Zheng, Yi Wang, Yixin Liu, Ming Li, Miao Zhang, Di Jin, Philip S. Yu, Shirui Pan. OpenAlex year 2026, venue IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering, citations 107, DOI 10.1109/tkde.2026.3680353. The taxonomy text was taken from the ar5iv HTML of arXiv:2202.07082, which OpenAlex lists as the open-access PDF, not from the IEEE PDF. — [ar5iv 2202.07082](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.07082); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tkde.2026.3680353)

### Inferences

- “Degree shift” in this literature is usually studied as degree bias of message passing, and the 2023–2024 mechanisms that change the graph at test time (GraphPatcher) or complete the graph (Grace) are the ones aimed at low-degree nodes. Subramonian et al. argue the bias is partly structural and not removed by expressive power or long training alone.
- Feature-missingness papers in this retrieval either separate feature and structure teachers (T2-GNN, 2023) or couple reconstruction and contrastive streams (MDS-GNN, 2024). No higher-cited 2025–2026 successor with an available abstract turned up under the title queries used.
- Homophily change is treated as a graph property (heterophily) with architectural filters, not as a named covariate “homophily shift” protocol in the titles retrieved.

## Is test-time batch-norm adaptation or temperature scaling used for GNNs under shift, and by whom?

### Takeaway

No retrieved 2023–2026 paper describes TENT-style test-time batch-norm statistic updates for GNNs under shift. Temperature scaling is used, but mainly as post-hoc calibration: homophily-aware (HoTS), wavelet-aware (WATS), entropy-driven for quantized GNNs, and a source-free global temperature (STAC) derived for calibration under distribution shift. Graph test-time adaptation in 2025–2026 instead uses entropy on prompts, spectral-energy refinement, gradient rotation, Riemannian prototype alignment, or data-side graph edits.

### Cited Findings

**Batch normalization**

- Title search `batch normalization graph neural` (2023–2026) did not return a test-time adaptation paper. BNM-CDGNN applies batch normalization to hidden layers after average pooling in a crystal-property GNN. Full title: “BNM-CDGNN: Batch Normalization Multilayer Perceptron Crystal Distance Graph Neural Network for Excellent-Performance Crystal Property Prediction.” Authors (OpenAlex): Kong Meng; Chenyu Huang; Yaxin Wang; Yunjiang Zhang; Shuyuan Li; Zhaolin Fang; Huimin Wang; Shihao Wei; Shaorui Sun. Year 2023. Venue: Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling. OpenAlex citations: 21. — [DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01148](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01148)
- Cherif et al. use batch normalization inside an encoder–decoder GNN “for reducing internal covariate shift and accelerating convergence” on credit-card fraud. That is the usual training-time role of batch normalization, not adaptation of BN statistics on an unlabeled shifted graph. Full title: “Encoder–decoder graph neural network for credit card fraud detection.” Authors (OpenAlex, first names returned): Asma Cherif; Heyfa Ammar; Manal Kalkatawi; Suhair Alshehri. Year 2024. Venue: Journal of King Saud University - Computer and Information Sciences. OpenAlex citations: 46. — [DOI 10.1016/j.jksuci.2024.102003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jksuci.2024.102003)
- Jin and Zhu mention batch normalization only as a precedent: normalization operators were proposed to alleviate oversmoothing, inspired by batch normalization in vision and language. That is a training-architecture discussion, not test-time adaptation. Full title: “Oversmoothing alleviation in graph neural networks: a survey and unified view.” Authors on ar5iv context: Yufei Jin and Xingquan Zhu (OpenAlex author list matches). Year 2025. Venue: Knowledge and Information Systems. OpenAlex citations: 14. Open-access HTML is arXiv:2405.01663. — [ar5iv 2405.01663](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.01663); [DOI 10.1007/s10115-025-02548-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-025-02548-6)

**Temperature scaling under shift or graph structure**

- Bahi gives a closed-form account of GNN calibration under distribution shift, argues that under homogeneous shift one global temperature is optimal, and proposes STAC, a source-free, label-free calibrator. Experiments on five real graphs are described as showing that calibration without target labels stays hard. Full title: “When does distribution shift break graph neural networks calibration?” Author (OpenAlex): Abderaouf Bahi. Year 2026. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 0. — [arXiv:2607.10804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.10804)
- HoTS assigns each node a positive temperature from logit entropy and estimated local homophily, with three temperature parameters, and does not change the predicted class. The abstract states best mean ECE of 4.79% across 18 node-classification benchmarks, two GNN backbones, and eight calibration baselines. Full title: “HoTS: Homophily-Aware Temperature Scaling for Graph Neural Network Calibration.” Authors (OpenAlex): Inwoo Tae; Yoontae Hwang; Yongjae Lee. Year 2026. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 0. The abstract is about calibration, not about restoring accuracy under covariate shift. — [arXiv:2609.32426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.32426)
- WATS sets node-specific temperatures from heat-kernel graph wavelet features, without retraining or neighbor logits. The abstract says lowest ECE among compared methods on seven datasets and two backbones, up to 42.3% ECE reduction versus baselines and 17.24% lower calibration variance versus graph-specific methods. Full title: “WATS: Calibrating Graph Neural Networks with Wavelet-Aware Temperature Scaling.” Authors (OpenAlex): Xiaoyang Li; Linwei Tao; Haohui Lu; Minjing Dong; Junbin Gao; Chang Xin Xu. Year 2025. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 0. — [arXiv:2506.23782](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23782)
- Jeddi and Nunez-Yanez use node-wise temperature from self-entropy, neighborhood entropy, and shortest-path distance to labeled nodes, for quantized GNNs, reporting ECE and NLL reductions of up to 95% and 66% without an accuracy drop after quantization. Full title: “Improving Reliability in Quantized Graph Neural Networks with Node-Wise Entropy-driven Temperature Scaling.” Year 2026. OpenAlex venue empty; DOI 10.23919/date69613.2026.11539433. OpenAlex citations: 0. This is quantization calibration, not distribution-shift adaptation. — [DOI 10.23919/date69613.2026.11539433](https://doi.org/10.23919/date69613.2026.11539433)
- MaxEnt Loss is a training loss for calibration on an in-distribution and out-of-distribution graph version of CelebA, not temperature scaling. Full title: “MaxEnt Loss: Calibrating Graph Neural Networks under Out-of-Distribution Shift (Student Abstract).” Author (OpenAlex): Dexter Neo. Year 2024. Venue: AAAI. OpenAlex citations: 1. — [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v38i21.30487](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i21.30487)

### Inferences

- If a reviewer asks whether GNNs copy image-domain TENT (update batch-norm, minimize entropy), the entropy half shows up in PromptDyG’s prompt objective, and a global temperature shows up in Bahi’s STAC, but the batch-norm half was not found.

## What do the newest surveys say are the open problems in GNN robustness?

### Takeaway

The surveys do not share one list. The 2024 Proceedings of the IEEE trustworthy-GNN survey asks for robustness metrics that are not tied to one attack, and for defenses that scale past medium graphs. The 2024 graph-distribution-shift survey asks for data-centric shift modeling, cross-modal graph construction under shift, harder near/far OOD evaluation, and trustworthy learning under shift. The heterophily survey (arXiv:2202.07082, OpenAlex TKDE 2026) asks for interpretability, scalable sampling that preserves heterophily, theory of generalization bounds, and link- and graph-level heterophily tasks. Oversmoothing surveys treat depth failure as related but not identical to adversarial robustness.

### Cited Findings

**Trustworthy GNNs, six aspects, robustness defenses by pipeline stage**

- Full title: “Trustworthy Graph Neural Networks: Aspects, Methods, and Trends.” Authors on ar5iv: He Zhang, Bang Wu, Xingliang Yuan, Shirui Pan, Hanghang Tong, Jian Pei. OpenAlex year 2024. Venue: Proceedings of the IEEE. OpenAlex citations: 145. DOI 10.1109/jproc.2024.3369017. Open-access HTML is arXiv:2205.07424. OpenAlex’s author string “Bang Ye Wu” does not match the ar5iv byline “Bang Wu.” — [ar5iv 2205.07424](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.07424); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/jproc.2024.3369017)
- Taxonomy of the whole survey: robustness, explainability, privacy, fairness, accountability, and environmental well-being. Robustness is split into attacks (threat models and attack methods) and defenses before training, during training, and after training. — same ar5iv HTML
- Open problems stated in section III-D: attack-deterioration and defense-rate numbers do not transfer to other attacks; robustness certificates depend on the GNN architecture and the graph type, which blocks comparison; the authors call for a quantitative metric that can be applied across architectures, graphs, and attack or defense methods, giving as an example an upper bound on prediction divergence in a domain. They also say most robust GNNs were only tested on medium graphs, that large-graph attacks exist, and that prior attacks and defenses often do not scale. — same ar5iv HTML, section III-D
- Section X says the survey highlights six trending directions. The ar5iv text names: (1) shift research from accuracy-only GNNs to trustworthy ones, including tradeoffs such as robustness versus environmental cost; (2) aspects beyond the six, explicitly including generalization and extrapolation; (3) cross-aspect relations, with counterfactual fairness described as conceptually close to robustness; (4) model-agnostic plug-in methods at pre-, in-, and post-processing; (5) a tools/datasets/metrics ecosystem, because i.i.d. toolkits do not carry over when edges couple nodes; (6) graph transformers, which the text says remain attackable and hard to explain, plus data-centric graph learning when bias sits in the graph itself. — same ar5iv HTML, section X

**Second trustworthy survey, four computational aspects**

- Full title: “A Comprehensive Survey on Trustworthy Graph Neural Networks: Privacy, Robustness, Fairness, and Explainability.” Authors (OpenAlex): Enyan Dai; Tianxiang Zhao; Huaisheng Zhu; Junjie Xu; Zhimeng Guo; Hui Liu; Jiliang Tang; Suhang Wang. Year 2024. Venue: Machine Intelligence Research. OpenAlex citations: 152. The abstract says each aspect has a taxonomy and a general framework, and that future directions are discussed, but the sub-taxonomy headings were not extracted from the PDF. — [DOI 10.1007/s11633-024-1510-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11633-024-1510-8)

**Distribution shift**

- Wu et al. (authors and arXiv id above) categorize graph learning under shift by whether the test distribution is observed and whether training labels suffice: graph domain adaptation, graph out-of-distribution learning, and graph continual learning.
- Domain adaptation taxonomy in the paper: semi-supervised domain adaptation, unsupervised domain adaptation, and test-time graph transformation.
- OOD taxonomy: generalization (further split in the headings into data augmentation, model development, and learning strategy), detection, and open-world graph learning (graph structure; knowledge graphs).
- Continual learning taxonomy: architectural, regularization, rehearsal, and hybrid.
- Future directions in section VIII, in the paper’s words: graph data-centric learning under distribution shifts (including predicting GNN performance under shift without test labels); cross-modality shift, including building graphs from images or text and then learning under that shift; evaluation protocols that cover near and far OOD (their example is train on citation networks and test on social networks) and metrics of graph discrepancy; the heading “Trustworthy Graph Learning Under Distribution Shifts” is present, but the paragraph body was cut off in the extract before its claims could be quoted. — [ar5iv 2402.16374](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.16374)

**Heterophily**

- Zheng et al. (authors and version caveat above) list these open directions in section VIII of the ar5iv HTML: interpretable heterophilic GNNs, because explanation subgraphs must use both near and far structure; scalable heterophilic GNNs, because mini-batch sampling can destroy heterophilic neighbor relations (they cite LINKX as evidence that an MLP can beat GNNs on large heterophilic graphs under mini-batch training); theoretical heterophily, including how heterophily changes generalization bounds, noting Ma et al. that GCN can work on heterophilic graphs under specific conditions; diverse tasks beyond node classification, especially heterophilic link and graph prediction; broader applications (biomedicine, chemistry, fraud, financial networks). The same paper’s discussion headings relate heterophily to adversarial robustness, over-smoothing, anomaly detection, and uncertainty, but those subsection bodies were not extracted. — [ar5iv 2202.07082](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.07082)

**Oversmoothing (adjacent, not an attack survey)**

- Rusch, Bronstein, and Mishra define oversmoothing as exponential convergence of a node-feature similarity measure, review mitigation, and say mitigation is necessary but not sufficient for expressive deep GNNs. Mitigation classes they name: normalization and regularization (explicit Dirichlet-energy constraints, DropEdge, Graph DropConnect, PairNorm, group and node normalization) and changes to discrete or continuous message-passing dynamics. Full title: “A Survey on Oversmoothing in Graph Neural Networks.” Year 2023. Venue: arXiv. OpenAlex citations: 84. — [ar5iv 2303.10993](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.10993)
- Jin and Zhu’s unified view, ATNPA, groups alleviation methods as residual-based, dense-based, random-mask, energy-based, diffusion-based, and transformer-based. The conclusion says many methods constrain embedding energy, while propagation has moved from one static adjacency toward learned or per-feature topology. They do not list a separate adversarial-robustness agenda. — [ar5iv 2405.01663](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.01663)

**OOD detection survey (2026), different problem**

- Nguyen et al. organize OOD detection into traditional statistical or deep families and then graph-aware GNNs, and state three working hypotheses: graph-aware transfer, structure-sensitive scoring, and hybrid objectives. Their case study GEO combines energy scoring with one-class regularization. Full title: “Conceptual Relationship Between Classical Machine Learning and Graph Neural Networks in Out-of-Distribution Detection: A Comprehensive Survey.” Authors (OpenAlex, truncated): Mao Nguyen; Thien Pham; Long Nguyen; Dang Le; Trung M. Pham; and further coauthors. Year 2026. Venue: IEEE Access. OpenAlex citations: 0. This is detection of inputs outside the training distribution, not a defense survey. — [DOI 10.1109/access.2026.3717452](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3717452)

**Graph test-time adaptation survey**

- Chen et al. (IJCAI 2026, citation above) state two challenges: data-level complex graph distribution shifts, and model-level limited information at test time. The abstract says the paper summarizes applications, benchmarks, and open opportunities. Those opportunities are not listed in the OpenAlex abstract, and the PDF body was not read.

### Inferences

## Which strongly cited 2024–2026 papers would a reviewer expect in a related-work section on “increasing GNN robustness,” beyond GNNGuard and Jaccard?

### Takeaway

Within this title-filtered OpenAlex slice, the papers with the highest citation counts that a robustness related-work section would be expected to acknowledge are the two 2024 trustworthy-GNN surveys (about 145–152 citations) and the heterophily survey (107 citations, with a 2022 arXiv id and a 2026 TKDE DOI). The next tier is smaller: SEC-GFD (57), PC-Conv (46), ES-GNN (22). The dedicated 2024 distribution-shift survey has only 3 citations, so it is topically central but not strongly cited. GNNGuard and Jaccard were not re-reviewed.
